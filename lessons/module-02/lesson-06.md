# 02.6 · Pelvic binder

By the end of this lesson you will be able to recognise the casualty who may be bleeding to death inside the pelvis, apply a commercial or bed-sheet pelvic binder at the right level, and then run the whole module as one drill: a blast casualty taken from the first tourniquet to a documented conversion.

> [!CAUTION]
> Performing these procedures on people without the required state certification is illegal.
> This lesson is for educational purposes only. Practise only on models and animal tissue.

## Why it matters

A broken pelvic ring can bleed litres into the space inside the pelvis and behind the abdomen, with nothing visible on the outside. Blast, a fall from height, a building collapse and a car crash all cause it. A binder squeezes the ring back toward its normal shape, which reduces the space blood can fill, holds the broken ends still, and helps clots stay in place.

TCCC added pelvic binders in 2016 (Change 16-02) because blast casualties with high leg amputations often had pelvic fractures that went unnoticed. The evidence that binders improve survival is not strong, but they are cheap, quick and low-risk when placed correctly. Placement is the weak point: in one study as many as 40% of binders were placed too high.

## What to do

### Who gets a binder (TCCC 2026)

Severe **blunt force or blast** injury **plus one or more** of:

- Pelvic pain.
- Any major lower-limb amputation or near-amputation.
- Physical findings suggesting a pelvic fracture: one leg shorter or rolled outward, bruising or swelling over the hips, groin, scrotum or perineum, blood at the tip of the penis.
- Unconsciousness.
- Shock.

You do not need to prove a fracture. Do **not** squeeze or rock the pelvis to test it; that can tear a clot loose. If the mechanism and one indication fit, apply the binder.

### Where it goes: the greater trochanters

The binder goes around the **greater trochanters**: the bony bumps on the outside of the upper thighs, roughly level with the crotch and the pubic bone. It does **not** go around the waist or the iliac crests (the "hip bones" where a belt sits). Too high, it squeezes the belly and does little to the pelvic ring.

To find them: put your palms on the outside of the casualty's upper thighs and slide upward until you feel the hard bump just below the hip joint. If you cannot feel them, line the binder up with the pubic bone at the front.

### Applying a commercial binder (SAM Pelvic Sling II or similar)

1. Empty the casualty's trouser pockets and remove a belt; objects under a binder cause pressure sores.
2. Slide the binder under the knees (the natural hollow), then work it **up** in a gentle side-to-side "sawing" motion until it is centred on the trochanters. Sliding it down from under the lower back tends to leave it too high. If you must lift, lift the back and thighs carefully rather than rolling the casualty far.
3. Bring both ends across the front. Feed the strap through the buckle and pull the ends in opposite directions until the buckle locks (the SAM AutoStop buckle clicks when the right force is reached).
4. Fasten the strap.
5. Put padding between the knees and ankles, and tie the knees and ankles loosely together with the feet pointing up or slightly inward.
6. Write the time on the card. Check distal pulses in both feet.
7. Leave it on. Binders are removed in hospital after imaging. In prolonged care, check the skin under the edges and over the bony points every few hours and pad any red areas.

A SAM Junctional Tourniquet belt (02.2) can serve as a pelvic binder too.

## Improvise it

A folded bed sheet is the standard improvised binder. The review behind TCCC Change 16-02 found that, in cadaver studies, a properly applied sheet reduced and held unstable fractures as well as commercial binders. In real use sheets vary more: one German registry linked sheets to higher bleeding deaths, possibly because less experienced centres used them. A single rescuer usually loses tension while securing a sheet, so use two people when you can.

**Sheet binder**

1. Fold a flat bed sheet lengthwise into a band about 20 cm wide (roughly a hand-span and a half).
2. Slide it under the knees and saw it up to the trochanters, as above.
3. Cross the ends over the front of the pelvis. Two people pull in opposite directions, firmly and steadily.
4. Secure it while still pulling: tie a reef knot, or fix the crossed layers with two or three cable ties punched through, large binder clips, safety pins, or several wraps of duct tape around the outside.
5. Pad and tie the knees and ankles loosely together.

**Other improvised binders:** a jacket with the sleeves tied across the front, a large towel, or a cargo strap over a folded blanket, all at the trochanter level.

**What fails:**

- **A belt or a narrow strap:** too narrow to spread pressure, and it ends up at the waist.
- **A sheet placed at the waist:** the most common mistake; it squeezes the belly, not the pelvis.
- **A loose knot:** sheets slip within minutes when the casualty is moved. Cable ties, tape or clips hold better than a knot alone.
- **Over-tightening with a windlass:** the TCCC review describes a military technique using cut trouser legs and a pole as a windlass, but it has not been studied, and twisting makes the force hard to judge. Tight enough to hold the pelvis, not crushing it; pulling firmly by hand with two people is easier to control.

**How to tell it is failing:** the band has ridden up to the waist, the knot has loosened, or the casualty's shock is getting worse. Re-centre and re-tighten. Worsening shock with a binder in place still means internal bleeding: the binder buys time, it does not stop arterial bleeding. This casualty is an urgent evacuation priority.

## When it goes wrong

- **Binder too high.** Re-check the level against the trochanters after every move.
- **Binder delayed while you test the pelvis.** The indications are enough. Do not squeeze or rock.
- **Pressure sores** in long holds: empty the pockets first, pad bony points, check the skin.
- **Missed pelvic fracture with a high amputation.** A blast amputation above the knee gets a binder.
- **Removing the binder to "check".** It stays until hospital imaging; removing it can restart bleeding.
- **Binder hides groin wounds.** Look at and treat groin and perineal wounds before the binder goes on.

## Scope and law

Pelvic immobilisation with a binder or sheet is within basic trauma care; the legal basis for lay aid (and why the Duty to Rescue Law gives no immunity) is in [lesson 01.5](../module-01/lesson-05.md). The tourniquet steps in the module drill below follow lessons 02.3-02.4: reassessment is expected of every trained responder, while conversion is a TCCC medic-level skill outside the Israeli EMT curriculum, to be done alone only when it is that or death and no one more qualified can be reached.

## Practice

> [!WARNING]
> Live partner only for fitting, not force: pull the pelvic sheet just snug. Tourniquets on a partner: healthy adult volunteer only (no clots, vascular disease, poor circulation, pregnancy or blood thinners), full occlusion for no more than about 1 minute at a time, never left unattended. All bleeding is simulated.

### Part 1: binder drill (20 minutes)

What you need: a partner lying on a mat in trousers, a flat bed sheet, three cable ties or duct tape, a small towel for padding, a marker.

1. Find both trochanters by touch; mark the level with tape on the trousers.
2. Fold the sheet to 20 cm, slide it under the knees and saw it up to the mark.
3. Cross, pull to snug with a helper, secure with ties or tape.
4. Pad and tie the knees and ankles.
5. Your partner rolls 30 degrees each way: check the sheet is still on the mark.

How you know it worked: from "go" to secured binder in under 3 minutes, centred on the trochanters (not the waist), still in place after the rolls.

### Part 2: module scenario - the full tourniquet lifecycle (70 minutes)

**Scenario.** 10:00. A drone strike hits a car park in a cut-off moshav. Your casualty, a 40-year-old man, has a fragment wound mid-thigh bleeding heavily and says his pelvis hurts. Drones are still overhead. Evacuation will not arrive before 15:00. You have one IFAK with two tourniquets, Combat Gauze, an Israeli bandage, a casualty card, a bed sheet and a phone.

What you need: a partner, two tourniquets (one may be improvised), a beef or lamb roast with a cut cavity taped to the partner's thigh as the wound, training gauze, an Israeli or elastic bandage, a bed sheet with ties, a casualty card (01.7), a marker, a phone timer. A second person reads out the time cues.

Run it in real time where marked, compressed elsewhere:

1. **Apply (direct threat, 10:00).** Apply tourniquet 1 "high and tight" over the trousers, snug only. Drag your partner 5 metres to "cover". Write "TQ 10:00" on the tourniquet and your partner's forehead.
2. **Reassess (indirect threat, cue: 10:20).** Expose the wound. Say whether a tourniquet is needed. Apply tourniquet 2 on the skin 2-3 inches above the wound; tighten until the foot pulse goes (no more than 1 minute), then loosen tourniquet 1, then release tourniquet 2 to snug. Say: "bleeding stopped, distal pulse absent". Record the reposition time.
3. **Pelvic binder (cue: 10:25).** Mechanism is blast, he has pelvic pain: apply the sheet binder at the trochanters, pad and tie knees and ankles. Record the time.
4. **Shock and criteria check (cue: 10:40).** The reader gives you: radial pulse strong, alert, you can stay with him, no amputation. State the three criteria and the time left in the 2-hour window (until 12:00 from the original 10:00 application).
5. **Convert (cue: 10:45).** Stage tourniquet 1 as the backup above. Pack the roast cavity, press 3 minutes by the timer, apply the pressure bandage with the pressure bar, release tourniquet 2 over 60 counted seconds. The reader calls either "dry" or "rebleed". On "rebleed": retighten, record, repack with fresh gauze, press, and try once more.
6. **Remove (cue: 11:15).** The dressing has stayed dry for 30 minutes. Remove the extra tourniquet 1 from the limb and record its removal time. Leave tourniquet 2 loosened in place and visible as the backup.
7. **Document and hand over (cue: 15:00).** Complete the casualty card: every tourniquet time (applied, repositioned, converted, re-applied if any, removed), hemostatic used, binder time, pulse and mental status checks. Give a 60-second MIST handover (01.7) to the "evacuation team", including "loosened tourniquet in place, conversion at ..., pelvic binder at ...".

How you know it worked:

- The repositioned tourniquet went on the skin before the first one was loosened, and the foot pulse was gone.
- The binder was at the trochanters and in place after you moved the casualty.
- You said all three conversion criteria and the 2-hour deadline before converting.
- Pressure was held a full 3 minutes and the release took a full minute.
- On a "rebleed" call you retightened within 10 seconds.
- The card has every time, and the handover mentions the loosened tourniquet, the conversion time and the binder.
- Run it three times, swapping roles; the third run should take under 20 minutes of hands-on time.
