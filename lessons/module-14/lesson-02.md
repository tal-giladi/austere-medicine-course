# 14.2 · Collapsed building: crush, long tourniquet, day-2 infection

By the end of this capstone you will have kept two casualties from a collapsed building alive for 48 hours: a man whose tourniquet you find already on for more than 2 hours, and a woman trapped under a slab for five hours. You will play every decision on a clock, from the first minute in the rubble through potassium danger, kidney injury, compartment syndrome and a wound that turns septic on day 2, to loading both onto one helicopter.

> [!CAUTION]
> Performing these procedures on people without the required state certification is illegal.
> This lesson is for educational purposes only. Practise only on models and animal tissue.

## Why it matters

A collapsed building is the situation where every module of Stage 2 meets: the tourniquet clock from module 2 has usually run out before anyone medical arrives, crush syndrome kills people who were talking when the weight came off ([08.1](../module-08/lesson-01.md)), and the casualties who survive the first day die on the third of kidney failure or infection unless someone keeps measuring urine and looking under dressings ([08.4](../module-08/lesson-04.md), [08.5](../module-08/lesson-05.md)).

Israel sits on the Dead Sea Transform fault, and missile strikes in the war have brought down residential buildings. In both, roads, hospitals and dialysis capacity are overwhelmed at the same time. The Module 8 scenario ([08.6](../module-08/lesson-06.md)) followed one crush casualty who recovered. This one adds a second casualty with a long tourniquet, a kidney injury that does not recover in the field, and a casualty point you must keep running for two days. The casualties and times are invented for training.

## What to do

### How to play

1. Print a flowsheet ([07.8](../module-07/lesson-08.md)) for each casualty and add a **urine column per hour** and a **girth column** for the injured legs. Print two casualty cards ([01.7](../module-01/lesson-07.md)) and a wall sheet.
2. Read one decision point at a time. Write your decision and the flowsheet entry within the **time limit**. Then open the reference decision.
3. Each reference names the lesson it comes from. Where yours differs, reread that part.
4. Each reference ends with the **household-only path**. Play it the second time through.

### Briefing

At **05:10** on a January morning a strong earthquake hits the Jordan Valley. In your town several apartment buildings have partly collapsed. Landslides have cut the roads; the regional hospitals are full. The Home Front Command says helicopter evacuation of the injured from your town will start in **about 48 hours**.

You are the medic of the neighbourhood emergency squad. At **08:00** the squad leader sends you to the casualty point the squad opened in the yard of the community centre, next to a collapsed four-storey building. With you: **Shira**, a nurse who can place IV lines and give IV drugs on a physician's order, who must leave at **13:00** for the field hospital; two helpers, **Gal** and **Omer**. A physician (**Dr. Levi**, on the Home Front Command medical line) can be reached by phone for 10 minutes every few hours.

### Decision points

**D1 08:00 (T+0:00). Arrival.** Aftershocks every few minutes. The squad set up the casualty point against the wall of the community centre, 6 metres from the collapsed building. **Time limit: 2 minutes.** What do you check first, and how do you organise the two casualties and your three helpers?

<details>
<summary>Reference decision</summary>

**Scene first** ([06.1](../module-06/lesson-01.md)): 6 metres from a partly collapsed building in an aftershock sequence is inside the collapse zone. Move the casualty point to open ground, away from any wall that could fall and from glass and power lines: the centre of the yard or the car park. Treating in the wrong place makes two more casualties.

**Four questions** ([01.1](../module-01/lesson-01.md)): time about 48 hours (band: 1-72 h); resources: the squad kit, Shira until 13:00, phone physician; threat: aftershocks, dust, possible gas leaks; responsibility: you.

**Roles** ([13.3](../module-13/lesson-03.md)): you lead and take Eli now; Shira goes to Rina's void with the rescue team (she can place the line); Gal writes the wall sheet and keeps the clock (tourniquet time 05:40, entrapment time 05:10); Omer sets up the treatment area (tarp, blankets, light, kit sorted by MARCH, a covered bucket and jugs for urine).

**Household-only path:** the same. Without a squad, your first job is still choosing a safe place to treat; a car park with blankets and a tarp is a casualty point.
</details>

**D1 08:05 (T+0:05). Eli's tourniquet.** You cut the jeans away. The CAT is mid-thigh over denim, tight. Mid-shin wound 4 cm with bone visible, no bleeding; the foot is pale and cool, no foot pulse. Radial pulse 112, weak. He is alert. Tourniquet time: **2 h 25 min**. **Time limit: 2 minutes.** Do you reposition it, convert it, or leave it?

<details>
<summary>Reference decision</summary>

**Leave it on and tight for now.** It is working (no bleeding, no distal pulse), and it has been on more than 2 hours. Repositioning it onto the skin would mean loosening the first one, which after 2 hours is a conversion, and TCCC 2026 says responders at your level should **not** convert beyond 2 hours **unless directed** by a paramedic or physician ([02.4](../module-02/lesson-04.md)). He is also in shock (fast, weak pulse), which fails the first conversion criterion anyway.

1. Check it is still tight; mark "05:40 (passer-by)" on it and on the card.
2. Card: **Urgent** ([01.7](../module-01/lesson-07.md)). Wall sheet: "TQ 2 h 25 at 08:05: call physician".
3. Treat the shock you can: lie flat, insulate and cover ([05.7](../module-05/lesson-07.md)). He has no abdominal injury and is alert, so start **oral fluid** (ORS, a cup every 15 minutes) so the kidneys have urine to flush with if the limb is later reperfused ([02.5](../module-02/lesson-05.md)).
4. **Open fracture** ([05.6](../module-05/lesson-06.md)): rinse off gross dirt with drinking water from a bottle with a hole in the cap, cover with a moist clean dressing and a dry layer; **cefadroxil 1 g** by mouth now ([04.6](../module-04/lesson-06.md)); note tetanus status. Splint loosely, with a window over the wound; do not try to push the bone back in.
5. Pain: acetaminophen **1 g**. No NSAIDs: a limb that may be reperfused after hours of ischaemia is a muscle-breakdown risk ([08.1](../module-08/lesson-01.md)).

**Household-only path:** the same decision. If the tourniquet had been a belt that was loosening (blood oozing, limb purple and swollen), the problem is a **venous** tourniquet: tighten it, or put a second one above it ([02.3](../module-02/lesson-03.md)). Do not loosen it.
</details>

**D1 08:20 (T+0:20). Rina in the void.** Through the void: she is alert, breathing 22, radial pulse 100 and strong, dusty. Her left arm is free. She has had nothing to drink since last night. Release is planned at about 10:30: about **5 h 20 min** after entrapment. **Time limit: 3 minutes.** Write her plan up to release, and how you share 4 L of saline between two casualties for two days.

<details>
<summary>Reference decision</summary>

**High crush risk**: thigh and calf, more than an hour, circulation cut off by the weight ([08.1](../module-08/lesson-01.md)). Permissive resuscitation does not apply.

1. **Shira places a cannula** in the left forearm.
2. **Saline before release** (ISN): **1 L/h**, but because extrication will take more than 2 hours, **reduce to 500 ml/h** after the first litre. So about 1 L by 09:20 and 500 ml/h to 10:30: about 1.6 L before release.
3. **Oral fluid alongside** (JTS: coached drinking): the **JTS crush home mix** (1 L water, 8 level teaspoons sugar, ½ teaspoon salt, ½ teaspoon baking soda), no potassium, a cup every 15 minutes through a straw or bottle. Not ORS with potassium once crush is likely ([08.1](../module-08/lesson-01.md)).
4. **The saline budget:** with 4 L in total, a nurse leaving at 13:00 and no monitor, the crush protocol **cannot be guaranteed** after release. That is the JTS condition for **two tourniquets side by side, high on the thigh, immediately before the slab is lifted** ([08.1](../module-08/lesson-01.md)). Keep about 2 L of saline for after release, and none for Eli unless he deteriorates (he can drink).
5. Dust mask or wet cloth, cover her for warmth, **no NSAIDs**, acetaminophen 1 g by mouth.
6. Pulse rate, **rhythm** and strength every 15 minutes. Prepare **calcium gluconate 10%** (needs a physician order) to be drawn up before release ([08.2](../module-08/lesson-02.md)).
7. Card: "Trapped R thigh + calf 05:10". Tell her, gently and only when she asks, about her husband; one helper stays to talk to her ([13.1](../module-13/lesson-01.md)). His body is covered, labelled and left for the rescue team to move.

**Household-only path:** no saline. Coached drinking of the home mix, about 1 L/h, a cup every 15 minutes, recorded; if she cannot drink enough, rectal home mix up to 500 ml/h through a soft tube ([08.1](../module-08/lesson-01.md)). Two tourniquets before release become even more important.
</details>

**D1 08:45 (T+0:45). The physician call.** You reach Dr. Levi. **Time limit: 3 minutes.** Write the call.

<details>
<summary>Reference decision</summary>

Script from [07.7](../module-07/lesson-07.md), photos of both flowsheets and the kit list sent first if the network allows. Headline: "Two casualties from a collapse. Eli, 30, 85 kg, open tibia fracture, tourniquet over clothes since 05:40, 3 hours now, was in shock, drinking. Rina, 58, 70 kg, right thigh and calf trapped since 05:10, release 10:30, saline 1 L/h via a cannula. I have a nurse until 13:00, 4 L saline, 3 calcium gluconate, ceftriaxone, cefadroxil, a salbutamol inhaler, no monitor." Questions, most important first:

1. "Do you direct conversion of Eli's tourniquet, and with what precautions?"
2. "Do you agree to tourniquets before Rina's release, and do you order calcium gluconate if her pulse slows or goes irregular?"

**Dr. Levi directs:** convert Eli if his radial pulse is strong and he is alert, with Shira's cannula in and saline ready, calcium drawn up, and pulse checks every 15 minutes for 2 hours after release; calcium gluconate 10% **10 ml IV over 2-3 minutes** for either casualty for a slowing or irregular pulse, repeat once after 5 minutes; call back at 11:30. Read back every item. Write the direction, time and name in Notes: this is your authority for the conversion ([01.5](../module-01/lesson-05.md)).

**Household-only path:** the call is the same and is now the most important thing you do: without a nurse or drugs, a physician's direction tells you whether to leave the tourniquet on.
</details>

**D1 09:00 (T+1:00). Converting Eli.** He has drunk 800 ml. Radial pulse 96, strong; alert. Tourniquet time **3 h 20 min**. Shira has placed a cannula in his right arm (saline lock). **Time limit: 3 minutes to list preparation and steps.**

<details>
<summary>Reference decision</summary>

Criteria now met: not in shock, wound watchable, not an amputation, and a physician has directed it in the 2-6 hour window ([02.4](../module-02/lesson-04.md), [02.5](../module-02/lesson-05.md)).

**Preparation:** calcium gluconate drawn up and labelled; saline bag ready on the lock (not running); timer; card; hemostatic gauze, plain gauze, pressure bandage; a **backup tourniquet staged** loosely above; acetaminophen given at 08:05; tell him it will hurt.

**Steps:** pack the wound around the bone end (never push bone in), packing proud of the skin, hold at least 3 minutes, pressure bandage, then **release over 1 minute**, leave the tourniquet loose in place. Card: "TQ 05:40 converted 09:08 on Dr. Levi's direction (08:45)."

**After:** pulse rate, rhythm and strength **every 15 minutes for 2 hours** ([08.2](../module-08/lesson-02.md)); urine colour and volume from now ([08.4](../module-08/lesson-04.md)); dressing for bleeding; the foot: colour, warmth, pulse, feeling, movement. The calf is now a **reperfused limb at risk of compartment syndrome**: start an hourly compartment exam with a girth mark ([08.3](../module-08/lesson-03.md)). Splint loosely with a window.

**Household-only path:** with no physician reached, the tourniquet stays on: past 2 hours a lay responder does not convert without direction ([02.4](../module-02/lesson-04.md)). Your job is warmth, oral fluid, pain relief, urgent evacuation and the call.
</details>

**D1 10:30 (T+2:30). Rina's release.** She has had 1.6 L of saline and 1 L of home mix. Two tourniquets went on high on her right thigh at 10:28, just before the jacks lifted the slab. She is carried out on a litter to the casualty point. The leg is swollen, cool, mottled. **Time limit: 2 minutes.** What happens now, and when do the tourniquets come off?

<details>
<summary>Reference decision</summary>

1. Away from the collapse site quickly, then a full primary survey ([08.1](../module-08/lesson-01.md)).
2. **Saline continues** at about 1 L/h; urine collection ready (bedpan or a wide container; every void measured and its colour recorded); calcium drawn up.
3. The crush tourniquets are **not for bleeding**; they hold back potassium. They are loosened **only when the crush protocol is running**: fluids going in, urine collection ready, calcium in hand, and she meets the conversion criteria (not in shock, can be watched) ([08.1](../module-08/lesson-01.md)). Here all of that is true by 10:45, and Dr. Levi already agreed: loosen slowly, leave them in place, write the time.
4. **Checks every 15 minutes** for the first 1-2 hours: pulse rate, rhythm, strength, mental state, breathing ([08.2](../module-08/lesson-02.md)). Watch the pulse oximeter's pulse rate and waveform continuously.

**Household-only path:** no saline running and no calcium. Course reasoning: without any way to treat high potassium, the case for leaving the crush tourniquets on until a physician directs otherwise is stronger; get the call before you loosen them, and keep coached drinking going.
</details>

**D1 10:55 (T+2:55). The pulse slows.** Ten minutes after loosening, Rina says her legs feel heavy. Her pulse falls from 108 to 54 and is irregular. **Time limit: 30 seconds.**

<details>
<summary>Reference decision</summary>

**Probable high potassium after release** ([08.2](../module-08/lesson-02.md)). On Dr. Levi's standing order, Shira gives **calcium gluconate 10% 10 ml IV over 2-3 minutes** now. Repeat after **5 minutes** if the pulse stays slow or irregular (you have 3 ampoules). Then:

- **Salbutamol**: the neighbour's inhaler through a bottle spacer, **10-20 puffs**, one at a time with 4-6 breaths each (course rule, an add-on only).
- Check glucose (glucometer).
- If it does not settle: **re-tighten the tourniquets** (JTS last resort) and call Dr. Levi.
- Keep saline running; record every dose and time.

In this run the pulse rises to 88 and regular after the first ampoule. Two ampoules are left: write it on the wall sheet.

**Household-only path:** no calcium. The only tools are re-tightening the tourniquets, the inhaler and the phone. This is why the tourniquets stay on in the household path until direction ([08.1](../module-08/lesson-01.md)).
</details>

**D1 13:00 (T+5:00). Shira leaves; Eli's calf.** Before leaving, Shira flushes both cannulas and hands over. Rina: 2.4 L saline in total; **1 bag left**; urine since release 210 ml in 2 hours, cola-coloured, dipstick blood +++. Eli: pain in the left calf 9/10 despite acetaminophen, much worse when you gently pull his toes up; the calf feels hard; girth up 2 cm; foot pulse present. Omer says: "Let me stitch Eli's wound closed before it gets infected, I have a sewing kit." **Time limit: 3 minutes.** Three problems: decide each.

<details>
<summary>Reference decision</summary>

**Rina's urine** ([08.4](../module-08/lesson-04.md)): myoglobin, and 105 ml/h is at the bottom of the crush target of **100-200 ml/h**. Keep the last litre of saline as **reserve** (for shock, or a route for drugs), run the lock flushed, and switch to **oral home mix about 1 L/h** (a cup every 15 minutes), adding rectal home mix up to 500 ml/h if she cannot drink enough ([08.1](../module-08/lesson-01.md)). Hourly volume and colour. Hourly overload check: breathing, crackles, puffy eyelids.

**Eli's calf** ([08.3](../module-08/lesson-03.md)): compartment syndrome after reperfusion is likely: pain out of proportion and on passive stretch, a tense compartment; a pulse does **not** exclude it. Loosen the splint and every bandage **down to skin**, re-splint loosely with padding, keep the leg **at heart level** (not raised), no ice, hourly exam with girth, photos and flowsheet to Dr. Levi now, and add "compartment syndrome" to his evacuation request. Fasciotomy is a surgeon's decision.

**Closing the wound** ([09.1](../module-09/lesson-01.md), [08.6](../module-08/lesson-06.md)): **no**. An open fracture with dirt, over 6 hours old, in a limb that may need surgery: it stays open, rinsed and dressed. Closing it traps infection. Give Omer a different job: he becomes the night fluids-and-urine recorder.

**Lines** ([07.5](../module-07/lesson-05.md)): write the cannula sites and times on the sheet; flush each lock **at least every 8 hours**. A working line is your only IV route after Shira leaves.

**Household-only path:** the urine and the calf are judged the same way, by jug and by hand. A household "sewing kit" closure is the most common harmful improvisation in a dirty wound: leave it open.
</details>

**D1 20:00 (T+12). The night.** Both are stable. Rina: urine 120-150 ml/h, getting lighter; pulse 84 regular. Eli: calf pain 6/10, girth stable, foot warm. You have been awake since 05:10. Three walking wounded from the squad's search have been brought to you. **Time limit: 5 minutes.** Write the night plan for the casualty point.

<details>
<summary>Reference decision</summary>

**Casualty point for days** ([13.3](../module-13/lesson-03.md)): re-triage everyone, walking wounded included; the walking wounded are treated, recorded and, if well, sent back with a safety-net instruction. Count supplies and write them on the wall: saline 1 L, calcium 2, ceftriaxone 4 g, cefadroxil 16 capsules.

**Rest** ([13.2](../module-13/lesson-02.md)): you sleep 21:00-01:00; Gal and Omer alternate. Wake card: pulse under 60 or irregular, pulse over 120, breathing over 30, confusion, urine under 100 ml/h (Rina) or under 43 ml/h (Eli, 0.5 ml/kg/h), dark or red urine getting darker, new breathlessness, blood through a dressing, calf pain rising or a cold foot.

**Nursing** ([07.4](../module-07/lesson-04.md)): turn and pad both every 2 hours; Rina's crushed leg is also a pressure area. Flush locks at 21:00 and 05:00. Mouth care. Food: Rina potassium-poor (white bread, rice, eggs; no bananas, tomatoes, dried fruit, salt substitutes; [08.2](../module-08/lesson-02.md)).

**Calls:** update Dr. Levi at the agreed time with both trends.

**Household-only path:** the same plan with a phone alarm for each 2-hour turn and the urine jugs labelled with the casualty's number.
</details>

**D2 08:00 (T+24). Rina's kidneys.** Overnight her urine fell: 60, 45, 40, 35 ml in the last four hours, very dark, despite about 1 L/h of home mix. Her eyelids are puffy. Breathing 24, SpO2 92% (96% yesterday), crackles at both lung bases. Pulse 92 regular. **Time limit: 2 minutes.**

<details>
<summary>Reference decision</summary>

**Acute kidney injury with fluid overload** ([08.1](../module-08/lesson-01.md), [08.4](../module-08/lesson-04.md)): urine under 100 ml/h for well over 2 hours at 1 L/h, and now overload signs. Pushing more fluid will drown her.

1. **Stop pushing fluid.** Give only what replaces measured losses plus a little, as ISN advises for oliguric victims; ask the physician for the exact daily allowance. Sit her up.
2. **Check the bladder** first (tender swelling above the pubic bone, [07.3](../module-07/lesson-03.md)), so you do not mistake retention for kidney failure.
3. **Call Dr. Levi now**: urine under 0.5 ml/kg/h for more than 2 hours and SpO2 falling are emergent triggers ([07.2](../module-07/lesson-02.md)).
4. **Evacuation for dialysis becomes her top priority**: tell the Home Front Command she is a crush casualty in kidney failure.
5. **Potassium will rise** as the kidneys fail (days 1-4, [08.2](../module-08/lesson-02.md)): checks every hour, pulse oximeter on, calcium ampoules counted, potassium-free food and drinks only (no ORS with potassium, no juice).
6. No NSAIDs, no furosemide unless a physician orders it for overload ([08.4](../module-08/lesson-04.md)).
7. Keep her saline lock patent: flush it on time.

**Household-only path:** identical. Fluid restriction needs no kit, only a measured jug and the discipline to stop.
</details>

**D2 18:00 (T+34). Eli's wound.** Temperature 38.9 °C, pulse 124, breathing 24, BP 104/70. He is muddled about the time. You open the dressing: grey-yellow pus with a foul smell; redness has spread 5 cm past this morning's pen line; the skin is red but not dusky; no crackling under the skin. Urine 25 ml in the last hour. His saline lock stopped flushing at 13:00 and was removed. **Time limit: 1 minute to decide, 5 minutes to list.**

<details>
<summary>Reference decision</summary>

**Sepsis from the open fracture** ([08.5](../module-08/lesson-05.md)): qSOFA 2 (breathing 22 or more, altered mental state), red flags (new confusion), and urine 25 ml/h is under 0.5 ml/kg/h (43 ml/h). Act **within the hour**.

1. **Call Dr. Levi and the evacuation line now**: Eli joins Rina as Urgent.
2. **Antibiotic now:** he cannot be trusted to swallow and has no IV, so **ceftriaxone 2 g IM** ([04.6](../module-04/lesson-06.md)): two 1 g vials, each mixed with 3.5 ml of 1% lidocaine, **two sites** (outer thigh of the uninjured leg and the ventrogluteal site), deep IM. Card: "ceftriaxone 2 g IM, two sites, 18:10". This replaces his oral cefadroxil. Metronidazole would be added for a foul wound if you had it: you do not; tell the physician.
3. **Fluids:** no IV. Oral ORS in sips, and **rectal** ORS starting at **100 ml/h**, up to **500 ml/h** ([08.5](../module-08/lesson-05.md)), targets: urine **0.3-0.5 ml/kg/h** (about 26-43 ml/h), radial pulse palpable, capillary refill 3 seconds or less, clearer mind. Watch for overload.
4. **Source control** ([08.6](../module-08/lesson-06.md)): pain relief first (acetaminophen 1 g), then irrigate with large volumes. Make **Dakin's 0.025%** from your 3.5% bleach: **7 ml per litre** of boiled, cooled water plus ½ teaspoon baking soda, labelled. Remove loose dead tissue and foreign material that lifts off easily; wet-to-dry dressing with Dakin's; mark the new redness edge with the time.
5. **Gas gangrene check** ([08.5](../module-08/lesson-05.md)): pain far beyond the wound, dusky or bronze skin, blisters with dark fluid, crackling under the skin: if any appear, it is a surgical emergency within hours: tell the physician those exact words.
6. Compartment check and potassium signs continue: sepsis and low urine both raise potassium.

**Household-only path:** no antibiotic. Source control is all you have: irrigation with large volumes of boiled, cooled water or 0.025% Dakin's from household bleach, removing pus and loose dead tissue, a honey or sugar dressing changed daily ([08.6](../module-08/lesson-06.md)), salty-sweet fluid by mouth and rectum, and an urgent evacuation request.
</details>

**D2 23:00 (T+39). Rina again.** Gal wakes you: Rina's pulse is 50 and irregular, weak. She feels weak and sick. Urine 20 ml in the last 2 hours. Her saline lock, placed D1 08:20, was flushed at 21:00 and runs freely. You reach Dr. Levi in 4 minutes. **Time limit: 1 minute.**

<details>
<summary>Reference decision</summary>

**High potassium from kidney failure** ([08.2](../module-08/lesson-02.md)): an at-risk casualty with a slow, irregular, weakening pulse needs calcium now.

- Calcium works only into a vein or bone. **Because the lock was flushed on time, you have a vein** ([07.5](../module-07/lesson-05.md)). Dr. Levi directs you to give **calcium gluconate 10% 10 ml slowly over 2-3 minutes** through it, flush before and after, and repeat once after 5 minutes if needed. Giving an IV drug is beyond your scope: this meets the last-resort test (dying, no one more qualified can reach her, a physician is directing you, and you have practised flushing and pushing through a lock on a model) ([01.5](../module-01/lesson-05.md)). Write it all in Notes.
- Salbutamol 10-20 puffs through the bottle spacer.
- No fluid boluses (she is overloaded); potassium-free food only.
- Glucose check.
- Escalate: she is now the most urgent casualty in the town for dialysis.

**Household-only path:** no calcium, no vein. The inhaler, potassium-free food and the phone. This is the point at which a kidney-failure casualty without dialysis dies, and why her evacuation request had to go in at 08:00 that morning.
</details>

## Improvise it

Play it a second time with no squad kit: no saline, no calcium, no antibiotics, no nurse. The decisions change more here than in 14.1, because two of the life-saving treatments (calcium and the first antibiotic) cannot be improvised.

| Step | Household way | What fails | How you see it failing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fluids before release (D1 08:20) | JTS crush home mix by mouth, a cup every 15 minutes; rectal home mix up to 500 ml/h through a soft tube ([08.1](../module-08/lesson-01.md)) | Slower than IV; a trapped casualty vomits or tires of drinking; rectal fluid runs back out | Cups refused, vomiting, fluid returning from the tube; dark scanty urine after release |
| Crush tourniquets (D1 10:30) | Two belts or cloth strips with stick windlasses, side by side, high on the thigh | Narrow straps cannot occlude a thick thigh | Still a pulse below; the limb swells and goes purple |
| Long tourniquet (D1 08:05) | Leave it on; call | Without a call it stays on to evacuation, and the limb may be lost | That is the accepted cost: life over limb ([02.5](../module-02/lesson-05.md)) |
| Urine (all) | Bedpan or a wide container for a woman, cut bottle for a man, a marked jug; colour against white paper | Missed voids, spills; no dipstick | Bladder full but "no urine"; check above the pubic bone |
| Potassium (D1 10:55, D2 23:00) | Re-tighten tourniquets; a salbutamol inhaler with a bottle spacer; potassium-free food | Nothing protects the heart like calcium | Pulse slows, becomes irregular, then stops: evacuate early instead |
| Compartment (D1 13:00) | Scissors to split every bandage to skin; leg at heart level | Nothing relieves the pressure except surgery | Pain rising despite analgesia, harder calf, numbness and weakness |
| Wound infection (D2 18:00) | Boiled water irrigation, 0.025% Dakin's from household bleach, honey or sugar dressing ([08.6](../module-08/lesson-06.md)) | No antibiotic: source control alone may not stop sepsis | Redness passing the pen line, fever, confusion, falling urine |
| Lines (D1 13:00) | None | No route for calcium | Plan earlier evacuation for anyone who may need IV drugs |

What the household path teaches: with no IV and no drugs, the decisions that save these two casualties are made early: tourniquets kept on until direction, early and repeated evacuation requests, fluids to a measured urine target, stopping fluids when the kidneys fail, and opening the dressing every day.

## When it goes wrong

| What goes wrong | Why it happens | Recovery |
|---|---|---|
| Treating next to the damaged building | Convenient wall, shelter from wind | Move to open ground before treating ([06.1](../module-06/lesson-01.md)) |
| Repositioning a 2.5-hour tourniquet "onto the skin" | Applying the reassessment rule without the clock | Past 2 hours, loosening is conversion: leave it, get direction ([02.4](../module-02/lesson-04.md)) |
| Lifting the slab before fluids and tourniquets | Rescue team ready, medic not | Agree the release time with the rescue leader; fluids and tourniquets first ([08.1](../module-08/lesson-01.md)) |
| ORS with potassium for a crush casualty with dark urine | Habit from diarrhoea care | Potassium-free home mix ([08.1](../module-08/lesson-01.md)) |
| Ibuprofen for crush or compartment pain | Only "good" painkiller in the drawer | No NSAIDs; acetaminophen, positioning, physician ([08.1](../module-08/lesson-01.md)) |
| Raising the swollen leg | First aid reflex for swelling | Heart level ([08.3](../module-08/lesson-03.md)) |
| Closing the open fracture wound | Wish to "protect" it | Leave open, irrigate, dress ([09.1](../module-09/lesson-01.md)) |
| More fluid for falling urine with crackles | The urine target overrides the lungs | Stop pushing, sit up, call; kidneys have failed ([08.1](../module-08/lesson-01.md)) |
| Unflushed lock, no route for calcium | Nursing tasks slip on night 1 | Flush every 8 hours as a written task ([07.5](../module-07/lesson-05.md)) |
| Antibiotic "at the next round" | Waiting for the morning | Within the hour; IM if no IV ([08.5](../module-08/lesson-05.md)) |

## Scope and law

Packing, pressure, tourniquets, positioning, warming, oral fluids, urine measurement, wound irrigation and dressing, splinting and documentation are lay or EMT first aid. Tourniquet conversion beyond 2 hours, IV cannulas and fluids, calcium, ceftriaxone injections and prescription antibiotics are nurse, paramedic or physician care in Israel. In this scenario they are given by a nurse or on a physician's direction, and the two times you act beyond your scope (pushing calcium through a lock, giving an IM antibiotic) are last resorts under the test in [01.5 · The law and your scope in Israel](../module-01/lesson-05.md), documented with who directed you and when. The Duty to Rescue Law gives no immunity.

## Practice

> [!WARNING]
> Never practise injections, cannulas or pushing drugs on a person. Use an injection pad, an orange or a chicken thigh for IM technique, and a practice saline lock taped to a sponge for flushing. Tighten tourniquets on a willing partner for under 2 minutes only.

**What you need:** two printed flowsheets with urine and girth columns ([07.8](../module-07/lesson-08.md)), two casualty cards, a wall sheet, a timer, a calculator, a training tourniquet, gauze and a pressure bandage, a pool noodle or rolled towel taped to a leg as the wound model, a tape measure, a 1 L jug, sugar, salt and baking soda with a calibrated teaspoon ([11.2](../module-11/lesson-02.md)), plain household bleach and a 10 ml syringe, an injection pad or chicken thigh with two syringes and needles, a practice saline lock on a sponge.

**Solo (about 3 hours):** each decision point on the timer; write the decision and the flowsheet entry before opening the reference. Do these physically: tourniquet check and conversion on the wound model (D1 09:00), mix 1 L of JTS crush home mix (D1 08:20), measure girth and mark the skin (D1 13:00), draw up and give "ceftriaxone" IM into the pad at two sites (D2 18:00), make 1 L of 0.025% Dakin's from your own bleach using the label strength (D2 18:00), flush the practice lock (D2 23:00). Say both handovers aloud, recorded, timed.

**With a partner (about 3.5 hours):** the partner plays both casualties and the physician, gives vital signs only when you check them, and may swap one card, for example "Rina passes no urine at all on D1 14:00" or "Eli's calf goes numb at D1 16:00".

### Scoring rubric

Score each point **2** (actions, order and reason match), **1** (right action, wrong order or reason missing) or **0** (missing, wrong, or over time).

| Decision point | Key items for 2 points |
|---|---|
| D1 08:00 | Casualty point moved out of the collapse zone; four questions; roles and the two clocks written |
| D1 08:05 | Tourniquet left on (over 2 h, no direction); shock care; open fracture rinsed, covered, cefadroxil 1 g; no NSAIDs |
| D1 08:20 | Saline 1 L/h then 500 ml/h; potassium-free oral mix; tourniquets before release justified; calcium prepared |
| D1 08:45 | Scripted call, two questions, read-back, direction documented |
| D1 09:00 | Criteria plus direction; backup tourniquet; slow release; 15-minute pulse checks; compartment watch started |
| D1 10:30 and 10:55 | Tourniquets loosened only with protocol running; calcium 10 ml over 2-3 min for slow irregular pulse; repeat rule |
| D1 13:00 | Saline reserved, oral to 100-200 ml/h; compartment steps (split to skin, heart level, no ice, call); wound left open |
| D1 20:00 | Re-triage; supplies counted; rest and wake cards; flush times; potassium-poor food |
| D2 08:00 | Kidney failure and overload recognised; fluids restricted; bladder checked; dialysis evacuation requested |
| D2 18:00 | Sepsis recognised; ceftriaxone 2 g IM two sites within the hour; rectal/oral fluids to urine target; Dakin's 7 ml/L; source control |
| D2 23:00 | Calcium through the flushed lock on direction; last-resort test named; no fluid bolus |
| D3 06:00 | Both packaged; tourniquets and lines shown; two MIST handovers under 30 s |

**Maximum 24.** How you know it worked: **19 or more**, no zero at D1 08:05, D1 08:20, D2 08:00 or D2 18:00, your 0.025% Dakin's matches the formula for your bleach (millilitres = 25 ÷ bleach %), and a stranger could follow both 48-hour courses from your flowsheets alone. Below 19, reread the lessons linked in the points you lost and run it again with the partner changing two cards.
