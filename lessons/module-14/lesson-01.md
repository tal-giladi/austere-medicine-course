# 14.1 · Safe room after an attack: 8 hours, 3 casualties, no evacuation

By the end of this capstone you will have run a safe room as a casualty point for 8 hours with three casualties and no evacuation: a woman with a tourniquet that has to be reassessed and converted, a child with a scalp wound and a scald, and an older man who walks in "fine" and is the one who deteriorates. Every step is timed, every decision has a reference answer that points back to the lesson that teaches it, and you play it twice: once with a home kit, once with household items only.

> [!CAUTION]
> Performing these procedures on people without the required state certification is illegal.
> This lesson is for educational purposes only. Practise only on models and animal tissue.

## Why it matters

Each module taught one skill in isolation. Real nights do not come in modules: the tourniquet clock, a crying child and a quiet older man all run at once, in a small room, with one kit and a phone that works sometimes. The skills that fail under that load are not the hands-on ones; they are the ones in between: noticing the clock, writing things down, rationing, re-checking the person who seemed fine, and making the call early.

This scenario is built on the patterns the course drew from 7 October and the war since ([01.4](../module-01/lesson-04.md)): limb bleeding controlled by a bystander's tourniquet, blast casualties who look well at first ([05.1](../module-05/lesson-01.md)), children among the injured ([05.8](../module-05/lesson-08.md)), and hours before anyone could reach the casualties ([01.1](../module-01/lesson-01.md)). The casualties and times are invented for training.

## What to do

### How to play

1. Print or copy the **wall sheet** and **three casualty cards** below, and a flowsheet from [07.8](../module-07/lesson-08.md) for each casualty.
2. Read the briefing and the kit list. Then read **one decision point at a time**. Do not read ahead.
3. At each point, write your decision (what, in what order, and the card or flowsheet entry) within the **time limit** shown. Then open the reference decision and mark yourself (scoring in Practice).
4. Your answers do not need to match word for word. They need the same **actions, order and reasons**. If yours differ, find the lesson linked in the reference and reread that part before going on.
5. Each reference ends with the **household-only path**: what you would do with no kit at all. Play it the second time through.

### Briefing

It is **19:30** on a winter evening in a town near the border. A rocket strikes the next building. Shattered glass and fragments come through the stairwell window of your building. Within five minutes, three injured people are in your family's safe room (mamad), 2.5 × 3 metres, with your spouse (Ruth), your 14-year-old daughter and a neighbour (Avi, 50, no medical training).

You phone MDA (101) at 19:36. The dispatcher says the area is closed because the fighting has not ended: **no ambulance can reach you for at least 8 hours**. The MDA doctor line and your health-fund doctor line work, with gaps. Sirens are expected to continue through the night.

You are the only person with medical training.

### Decision points

**T+0:00 (19:35). Arrival.** Three casualties, five people in a small room, one kit. Ruth is holding Noam. Avi is kneeling by Dana. **Time limit: 1 minute.** What are your four answers, and in what order do you see the three casualties?

<details>
<summary>Reference decision</summary>

Four questions out loud ([01.1](../module-01/lesson-01.md)): **Time**: at least 8 hours, plan for the 1-72 hour band. **Resources**: the home kit above, no IV, no evacuation. **Threat**: more rockets likely; you are already in the protected room, so this is indirect-threat care ([01.2](../module-01/lesson-02.md)). **Responsibility**: you, alone.

Order by circulation first ([01.3](../module-01/lesson-03.md)): **Dana first**, because a tourniquet over clothing on a thigh with soaked jeans may not be controlling an arterial bleed. **Noam second**, but in parallel: give Ruth a gauze roll and tell her to press hard on the scalp wound and hold Noam still on her lap. Scalp wounds bleed heavily but almost always stop with firm pressure ([02.2](../module-02/lesson-02.md)). **Moshe third**: walking, talking, but a blast casualty, so he is not "minor" until surveyed ([05.1](../module-05/lesson-01.md)). Ask Avi to sit Moshe against the wall, away from the door, and to tell you at once if he becomes breathless or confused.

Set up the room in one minute ([01.4](../module-01/lesson-04.md)): plastic sheet and blankets on the floor, kit opened onto a clean tray sorted by MARCH, headlamp on, one person (your daughter) on the phone and writing times on the wall sheet.

**Household-only path:** the same order. Ruth's pressure on the scalp uses a folded clean towel. Your daughter writes times on masking tape on the wall.
</details>

**T+0:05 (19:40). Dana's tourniquet.** You cut the jeans away. The wound is 3 cm wide, mid-thigh, front. Blood still oozes steadily from it. The CAT is high on the thigh, over the jeans' thick seam. The pulse behind her inner ankle is present. Radial pulse 112, weak; she is alert and frightened. **Time limit: 3 minutes.** What do you do, and what do you write?

<details>
<summary>Reference decision</summary>

The tourniquet is not working as an arterial tourniquet: bleeding continues **and** the distal pulse is present ([02.3](../module-02/lesson-03.md)). It may be acting as a venous tourniquet and making the bleeding worse.

1. Apply a **second CAT directly on the skin, 5-7.5 cm (2-3 inches) above the wound**, tighten until the bleeding stops **and** the ankle pulse is gone.
2. Then **loosen the first tourniquet** and leave it in place, loose, as a backup.
3. Watch the wound for a minute.
4. Mark the time on the new tourniquet and on the card.

Card, Tourniquets: "R leg: CAT #1 over clothes 19:33 (Avi), loosened 19:41. CAT #2 on skin 19:41". Vital-sign row: "19:40 P 112 weak radial, A&O, pain 8".

She has shock signs (fast, weak pulse) after a major bleed. Shock means the first conversion criterion is **not met** now ([02.4](../module-02/lesson-04.md)); recheck in 15-30 minutes. TXA would be indicated if you had IV access ([04.3](../module-04/lesson-03.md)); you do not: tell MDA so it is ready on arrival. Lay her flat, insulate her from the floor and cover her ([05.7](../module-05/lesson-07.md)). The clock that matters now is **2 hours from 19:33**: conversion must happen by **21:33** without a physician's direction ([02.4](../module-02/lesson-04.md)). Write "convert by 21:33" on the wall sheet.

**Household-only path:** if the only tourniquet was improvised, the same check applies, and an improvised one fails more often: if a belt with a spoon windlass does not stop the pulse, add a second one above it, side by side ([02.3](../module-02/lesson-03.md)). A tourniquet you cannot tighten enough is a venous tourniquet: worse than none. Then pack and press instead ([01.3](../module-01/lesson-03.md)).
</details>

**T+0:15 (19:50). Noam.** Ruth's pressure has nearly stopped the scalp bleeding. The scald: the whole outer half of his left forearm is red with large blisters, and there is a blistered patch on the left chest the size of about three of his palms. He is still screaming; nobody has cooled the burn. He did not lose consciousness. **Time limit: 3 minutes.** Size the burn, and say what you do for the scalp, the burn and the boy.

<details>
<summary>Reference decision</summary>

**Scalp:** keep firm pressure 10 minutes; then a pressure bandage around the head (Israeli bandage with the pressure bar over the wound) ([02.2](../module-02/lesson-02.md)). Closing it can wait: a clean scalp wound can be closed later with hair apposition or strips ([09.2](../module-09/lesson-02.md)), and you will look at it properly once the room is calm.

**Burn size** ([05.4](../module-05/lesson-04.md)): count only the blistered (partial-thickness) skin. His palm with fingers is about 1%. The chest patch is about 3 palms, so **3%**. Lay his palm on the forearm blisters: they cover about 3 palms too, so **3%**. Total about **6% TBSA**; with the uncertainty of palm counts in a moving child, plan for **8%** and write "estimate". Either way it is under 10%.

**Burn care:** it is minutes old, small and not on a large area, so **cool it with cool running or poured water for 20 minutes** ([05.4](../module-05/lesson-04.md)), from your stored water, while keeping the rest of him warm. Then **cling film laid loosely** in strips, never wrapped round the arm. Take off his watch or bracelet now, before the arm swells.

**Fluids:** under 10% TBSA in a child usually needs about **1.5 × maintenance and drinking**, not a formula ([05.4](../module-05/lesson-04.md)). Maintenance for 25 kg is about **65 ml/h** ([07.3](../module-07/lesson-03.md)), so offer about **100 ml/h** of ORS or diluted juice with a pinch of salt. Urine target **1 ml/kg/h = 25 ml/h**.

**Pain** (weight 25 kg, [05.8](../module-05/lesson-08.md), [04.5](../module-04/lesson-05.md)): acetaminophen **15 mg/kg = 375 mg** (¾ of a 500 mg tablet, crushed in jam or juice), and ibuprofen **10 mg/kg = 250 mg** (one 200 mg tablet plus a quarter is close; 200 mg is acceptable). Card both with times. Ibuprofen is reasonable here: he is not bleeding badly now, not in shock, and is drinking.

**The boy:** give Ruth one job, holding him and talking, and keep him with her. Calm, plain words about what will happen next ([13.1](../module-13/lesson-01.md)).

Card: "Noam, 8 y, ~25 kg. Scalp lac R, pressure dressing 20:00. Scald L forearm + L chest ~8% (est.) 19:30, cooled 19:50-20:10, cling film. Acetaminophen 375 mg PO 19:55; ibuprofen 200 mg PO 19:55."

**Household-only path:** cooling uses tap water from a jug; cling film from the kitchen is the burn dressing. If there is no cling film, a clean, dry pillowcase. Do not use toothpaste, butter, oil or ice. Pain relief from a children's syrup: read the strength on the bottle and calculate the 375 mg dose from it ([04.7](../module-04/lesson-07.md)); never estimate "a spoonful".
</details>

**T+0:25 (20:00). Moshe.** You strip him to the waist in the warmest corner. Four small fragment wounds on his back and right arm, not bleeding. Blood in the right ear canal; he hears you only if you shout. Breathing 18, SpO2 96%, pulse 68 regular, BP 146/88 (he says his usual is about 150/90). Belly soft, no pain. **Time limit: 2 minutes.** What category is he, and what is his plan?

<details>
<summary>Reference decision</summary>

**Priority, not routine**, because of the mechanism: he was close to the window, indoors, with a burst eardrum. A ruptured eardrum is a clue, not a test, for blast lung and bowel injury ([05.1](../module-05/lesson-01.md)).

1. Full head-to-toe survey, including back, buttocks, armpits, groin and scalp; mark every wound on the card outline.
2. Ear: do not clean, irrigate or plug the canal; cover loosely. Face him when you talk; write instructions down for him.
3. Write the blast details on the card: distance, indoors, ear symptoms, breathing rate, SpO2, pulse, abdomen.
4. Plan: **breathing, pulse, SpO2 and abdomen every hour for 6 hours**, then every 4 hours ([05.1](../module-05/lesson-01.md)).
5. **Two warnings on the wall sheet:** "bisoprolol: pulse may not rise in shock" and "apixaban: bleeds more, no NSAIDs". A beta-blocker hides the fast pulse you rely on, so you will watch blood pressure, skin, mental state and urine instead ([04.1](../module-04/lesson-01.md)).

Card row: "20:00 P 68 reg, BP 146/88, RR 18, SpO2 96%, A&O, abdomen soft non-tender, R ear blood, hears only shouting."

**Household-only path:** no oximeter or cuff. Count breathing and pulse for a full 30 seconds, check capillary refill, skin colour and warmth, and ask the same orientation questions every hour. Write them; the trend is your monitor ([07.2](../module-07/lesson-02.md)).
</details>

**T+0:40 (20:15). Cards, the call and drugs.** It is quieter. Dana: pulse 104, radial stronger, alert, pain 8. Noam: calmer, drinking. Moshe: unchanged. You can reach the MDA doctor line. **Time limit: 5 minutes.** Write your call, and decide the drugs for all three from the kit.

<details>
<summary>Reference decision</summary>

**Call** with the script from [07.7](../module-07/lesson-07.md): who you are and your number; headline ("three casualties after a rocket strike, one thigh tourniquet since 19:33, a child with an 8% scald, a 67-year-old blast casualty on a blood thinner; no evacuation for 8 hours"); kit (no IV, no TXA, no opioids, cefadroxil, acetaminophen, ibuprofen, ORS); **one question**: "I plan to convert the tourniquet before 21:33 if she is out of shock; do you agree, and what should make me call back?" Read back the answers. Write the call in the Notes.

**Drugs:**

| Casualty | Drug | Why | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dana | Acetaminophen **1 g** PO now (next 6 h later) | Pain; no bleeding risk | [04.5](../module-04/lesson-05.md) |
| Dana | Cefadroxil **1 g** (2 × 500 mg) PO now, once daily | Open fragment wound; she can swallow and is no longer in shock | [04.6](../module-04/lesson-06.md) |
| Dana | Hold ibuprofen for now | Fresh major bleed, conversion ahead, urine output unknown (course reasoning, as in [07.8](../module-07/lesson-08.md)) | |
| Moshe | Cefadroxil **1 g** PO now | Open fragment wounds | [04.6](../module-04/lesson-06.md) |
| Moshe | Acetaminophen 1 g only if he needs it; **no ibuprofen** | Apixaban: NSAIDs add bleeding risk | [04.5](../module-04/lesson-05.md) |
| Noam | None more now | Acetaminophen and ibuprofen given at 19:55; no antibiotic for a clean scald or a scalp wound | [04.6](../module-04/lesson-06.md) |

Check allergies first and write them. Count stock: cefadroxil 12 capsules minus 4 = **8 left** (enough for 2 more days for both adults). Wall sheet: "Next acetaminophen Dana 02:15, Noam 23:55".

**Household-only path:** no antibiotic. The open wounds are rinsed with plenty of clean drinking water from a syringe or a pierced bottle and covered ([08.6](../module-08/lesson-06.md)); you write "no antibiotic available" so the hospital gives the first dose on arrival.
</details>

**T+1:10 (20:45). Converting Dana's tourniquet.** Dana: radial pulse 96, strong; alert and answering clearly; the wound is exposed under your headlamp. The doctor agreed on the call. Tourniquet #2 has been on since 19:41 (the first one since 19:33). **Time limit: 3 minutes to decide and list your preparation; then do it on a model (Practice).**

<details>
<summary>Reference decision</summary>

**All three criteria are met** ([02.4](../module-02/lesson-04.md)): not in shock (strong radial pulse, normal mental state), you can watch the wound, and it is not an amputation. Time since first application **1 h 12 min**: under 2 hours, convert now.

Preparation: acetaminophen given at 20:15; hemostatic gauze (one) and a plain roll; a pressure bandage; a **backup tourniquet staged loosely above** the one in use; timer, light, card. Explain to Dana that it will hurt.

Steps, in skill-card order ([02.4](../module-02/lesson-04.md)): expose, pack the wound tightly with the hemostatic gauze, packing 2.5-5 cm proud of the skin, **hold 3 minutes**, pressure bandage over the pad, wrapped tight with the pressure bar, secure, then **release the tourniquet slowly over 1 minute** while watching the bandage. Leave it **loose in place**.

Card Notes: "TQ #2 converted 20:52: Combat Gauze + Israeli bandage. TQ loosened, left in place. Ankle pulse felt 20:58."

Afterwards: check the dressing every few minutes for 30 minutes, then every 15 minutes and after every move; ankle pulse, colour and warmth; pain; urine colour when she passes urine ([02.4](../module-02/lesson-04.md)). Keep one hemostatic gauze in reserve for a re-bleed (that is the second one; your kit had 2).

**If it re-bleeds at release:** re-tighten the tourniquet until the bleeding stops and the pulse is gone, write the time, and as a course rule you may try once more with fresh packing while still inside 21:33 ([02.4](../module-02/lesson-04.md)).

**Household-only path:** pack with clean cotton strips torn from a T-shirt, pushed in tightly with a finger, press at least 3 minutes (with plain cloth, holding longer costs nothing: course reasoning), then a folded towel bound tight with a strip of sheet. Release the improvised tourniquet slowly. Plain cloth controls less bleeding than hemostatic gauze: watch it more often, and expect to need the tourniquet again.
</details>

**T+2:30 (22:05). Siren.** A long siren. Everyone is in the safe room; the door is closed. It is 9 °C outside and the room is cold. Avi suggests bringing in his camping gas stove to make tea and warm the room. Someone else lights two candles. **Time limit: 30 seconds.**

<details>
<summary>Reference decision</summary>

**No stove, no candles.** Never run a gas stove, heater, charcoal or generator in a sealed safe room; candles burn oxygen and are a fire risk ([06.5](../module-06/lesson-05.md)). Put the candles out. Check the battery CO alarm is on. Warmth comes from layers: casualties on insulation from the floor, blankets under and over, foil blankets inside the wrap, hats, a sleeping bag for Dana, the vapour barrier outermost ([05.7](../module-05/lesson-07.md)). Warm sweet drinks made from the kettle **outside** the safe room between sirens, if it is safe to leave. Several people with a headache in a closed room means everyone out to fresh air first ([06.5](../module-06/lesson-05.md)).

**Household-only path:** the same. Hot-water bottles help if they are filled with hot (not boiling) water, wrapped, placed on the armpits and chest over a thin layer, never on bare skin, a tourniquet limb or burned skin, and checked every 15-20 minutes ([05.7](../module-05/lesson-07.md)).
</details>

**T+3:00 (22:35). Moshe changes.** On the hourly check Moshe says he feels sick and has a "stomach ache". Pulse 74 regular. BP 118/76. Breathing 20. SpO2 95%. His skin is cooler than an hour ago. His belly is tender below the navel; no visible bruise. **Time limit: 2 minutes.** What do you think, and what changes?

<details>
<summary>Reference decision</summary>

Read the **trend**, not the numbers: his systolic has fallen from 146 to 118 (about 30 mmHg below his normal), skin cooler, new abdominal pain and nausea after blast. His pulse barely rose **because of bisoprolol**. On apixaban, any internal bleed is worse. This is a **delayed blast abdomen until proven otherwise** ([05.1](../module-05/lesson-01.md)).

1. **Upgrade him to Urgent** on the card and the wall sheet ([01.7](../module-01/lesson-07.md)).
2. **Nothing by mouth** except small sips for comfort; no more oral drugs unless needed ([05.1](../module-05/lesson-01.md), [05.5](../module-05/lesson-05.md)).
3. Lay him flat or in a position of comfort, keep him warm.
4. Checks every 15 minutes: BP, pulse, skin, mental state, abdomen (girth with a tape at the navel, marked on the skin).
5. **Call now**: the drop in pressure and new abdominal signs after blast is an emergent reason to call ([07.2](../module-07/lesson-02.md)). Tell MDA he needs a surgeon and that he takes apixaban (a hospital may give a reversal agent).
6. Bring Avi in as his watcher; tell him what to report.

**Household-only path:** no cuff. You see the same story in **skin** (cool, pale, sweaty), **mental state** (slower answers), **capillary refill** (slower), a **radial pulse that becomes weaker** even if not faster, and **less urine**. On a beta-blocker these are your shock signs ([04.1](../module-04/lesson-01.md)).
</details>

**T+4:30 (00:05). Night plan.** Dana: pulse 90, dressing dry, ankle pulse present, pain 6, has passed 150 ml of dark yellow urine since 20:00 (bottle). Noam: asleep, vomited once at 23:40, woke easily, knew his name and where he was; urine 1 nappy-sized void. Moshe: pulse 76, BP 112/72, abdomen still tender, girth unchanged. You have been awake since 06:00. **Time limit: 5 minutes.** Write the plan for the next 3 hours.

<details>
<summary>Reference decision</summary>

**Fluids and urine** ([07.3](../module-07/lesson-03.md)): Dana's target 0.5 ml/kg/h = **33 ml/h**; 150 ml in 4 hours is 37 ml/h, just on target and dark: push ORS (about 200 ml every 30-60 minutes). Now that she is out of shock and passing urine, ibuprofen 400 mg with food is reasonable (course reasoning). Noam: keep about 100 ml/h of ORS when awake; measure urine in a jug.

**Noam's vomit:** he hit his head and has a scalp wound. One vomit, waking easily and oriented is not a red flag by itself, but it puts him on **neuro checks**: wake him every hour for 3 hours to check he knows you, squeezes both hands, pupils equal ([05.2](../module-05/lesson-02.md)). Red flags: a second or third vomit, a headache that worsens, drowsiness, confusion, unequal pupils, a fit. Any of these: call, and he becomes Urgent.

**Nursing** ([07.4](../module-07/lesson-04.md)): turn and pad Moshe and Dana every 2 hours; urine bottle for each; mouth care.

**People** ([13.2](../module-13/lesson-02.md), [13.3](../module-13/lesson-03.md)): you cannot stay sharp for 8 more hours alone. Sleep in 90-minute blocks. Ruth watches Noam and Dana; Avi watches Moshe. Write a **wake card** for each watcher: Dana: blood through the dressing, foot cold or numb; Noam: vomiting again, hard to wake, confused; Moshe: more pain, faster breathing, confusion, fainting, sweating. "Wake me for any of these, or if you are worried."

**Household-only path:** the same plan. A measured jug or a marked 1.5 L bottle replaces the urine bottle; a kitchen timer or phone alarm sets the turns.
</details>

**T+6:00 (01:35). Moshe is worse.** Avi wakes you. Moshe is grey and sweaty, slow to answer, and says he is thirsty. Pulse 88. BP 90/62. Breathing 24. His belly is more swollen (girth up 3 cm) and very tender. **Time limit: 1 minute.** What do you do?

<details>
<summary>Reference decision</summary>

**Shock from an internal abdominal bleed** (shock index 88 / 90 = about 1.0, falling pressure, altered mental state, rising girth) in a man whose pulse cannot rise. There is nothing in your kit that stops this bleed: only a surgeon can ([05.5](../module-05/lesson-05.md)).

1. **Emergent call** to MDA: shock index about 1, systolic falling, altered mental state ([07.2](../module-07/lesson-02.md)). Ask for **any** vehicle the army or police can send for one urgent casualty. Give MIST.
2. Keep him flat, warm (full wrap), nothing by mouth.
3. **Fluids:** you have no IV, and oral fluid is wrong for a casualty with a suspected bowel injury ([05.1](../module-05/lesson-01.md)). Your only job is to get him moving.
4. **Prepare to move** ([07.8](../module-07/lesson-08.md), [06.2](../module-06/lesson-02.md)): card complete and taped to his chest, his medication list (bisoprolol, apixaban, last doses), a blanket drag or a short litter for the stairs, four carriers.

MDA calls back at 01:50: an army ambulance will reach the building entrance at 02:20. Moshe goes first: he is the most urgent, and the only one whose problem worsens every minute you wait.

**Household-only path:** the same decisions. A blanket drag with two people on each side gets him down the stairs; a door is too wide for most stairwells ([06.2](../module-06/lesson-02.md)).
</details>

**T+6:45 (02:20). Handover of Moshe.** The army medic has 30 seconds. **Time limit: 30 seconds, out loud.**

<details>
<summary>Reference decision (example)</summary>

"Moshe, 67, about 80 kg. **Urgent.** Blast from a rocket at 19:30, indoors by a window. Right eardrum burst. Small fragment wounds on back and right arm. Fine for 3 hours, then belly pain from 22:35; now **shock**: BP from 146 to 90, pulse only 88 because he is on **bisoprolol**, grey, confused, girth up 3 cm. He takes **apixaban**, last dose this morning. Given cefadroxil 1 g at 20:15, nothing since, nil by mouth from 22:35. No allergies. Card is on his chest." ([01.7](../module-01/lesson-07.md))
</details>

**T+8:00 (03:35). MDA arrives for Dana and Noam.** Dana: pulse 84, dressing dry, foot warm with a pulse, urine 160 ml since midnight. Noam: woke normally at every check, no more vomiting. **Time limit: 2 minutes.** Who goes first, in what category, and what goes with them?

<details>
<summary>Reference decision</summary>

**Dana: Priority** (converted tourniquet, now stable, open wound, needs surgery within hours). **Noam: Priority** (child with an 8% scald and a head injury with one vomit), and he stays with his mother: they go in the same ambulance if possible. Hand over Dana first, then Noam, each with MIST under 30 seconds ([01.7](../module-01/lesson-07.md)).

Package ([07.8](../module-07/lesson-08.md)): flowsheets and cards in a zip-lock bag; the loose tourniquet left on Dana's thigh and pointed out ("TQ on 19:33, converted 20:52, loose, in place"); drug strip with times and next due; Noam's burn and neuro check record; the call log. Tell the crew what you could not give (TXA, IV fluid).

Then: restock the kit list on the wall, write what you used, eat, sleep. Talk through the night with Ruth and Avi tomorrow ([13.2](../module-13/lesson-02.md)).
</details>

## Improvise it

Play the scenario a second time with **only the household column** of the kit table. The decisions stay the same; the tools, the failure points and how often you check change. This is the thread through every step:

| Step | Household way | What fails | How you see it failing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tourniquet (T+0:05) | Belt or folded cloth 4-5 cm wide with a wooden spoon windlass, tied off ([01.2](../module-01/lesson-02.md)) | Narrow or stretchy material does not stop arterial flow; the windlass unwinds | Bleeding continues, the foot pulse stays, the limb goes purple and swollen (venous tourniquet) |
| Packing and pressure (T+1:10) | Torn cotton strips packed tight, towel and a strip of sheet bound hard | Less clotting than hemostatic gauze; the binding loosens with movement | Blood through the outer layer; check every 5 minutes, not 15 |
| Burn cover (T+0:15) | Kitchen cling film in loose strips; clean pillowcase | Wrapped tightly it becomes a tourniquet as the arm swells | Fingers pale, cold or tingling; loosen at once |
| Monitoring (all) | Watch with a second hand, a written flowsheet, the same person checking | No BP or SpO2 numbers; a beta-blocker hides the pulse | Skin, mental state, capillary refill and urine change before you have a number |
| Warmth (T+2:30) | Duvets under and over, rubbish bags as vapour barrier, hats | Floor cold comes through thin layers; hot-water bottles burn | Shivering, cold hands and feet, a casualty who stops shivering but becomes drowsy |
| Fluids (T+4:30) | WHO home ORS from sugar and salt with calibrated spoons ([11.2](../module-11/lesson-02.md)) | Heaped spoons make it too salty; plain water causes low sodium | Vomiting, a solution saltier than tears; check the taste |
| Urine (T+4:30) | Cut-down 1.5 L bottle or a jug marked from a measured 200 ml cup ([07.4](../module-07/lesson-04.md)) | Spills and missed voids make output look falsely low | Bladder full above the pubic bone but "no urine" |
| Antibiotic (T+0:40) | None | Infection risk rises over days, not hours | Not a problem tonight; it is the hospital's first dose: write it on the card |
| Moving (T+6:00) | Blanket drag, four carriers, a sheet folded as handles ([06.2](../module-06/lesson-02.md)) | Blankets tear, carriers drop the head end on stairs | Head-end carrier calls every step; check the casualty after the move |

What cannot be improvised tonight: blood, a surgeon for Moshe's abdomen, and IV fluids. Your household-only job for him is the same as with a kit: see the trend early and get him moving.

## When it goes wrong

| What goes wrong | Why it happens | Recovery |
|---|---|---|
| Treating Noam first because he is screaming | Noise draws attention; circulation-first is forgotten | Hand Noam to Ruth with a gauze and a job, go back to the thigh ([01.3](../module-01/lesson-03.md)) |
| Leaving the over-clothes tourniquet because "it's on" | The distal pulse is not checked | Expose, check the pulse, second tourniquet on skin, loosen the first ([02.3](../module-02/lesson-03.md)) |
| Missing the 2-hour conversion window | Nobody wrote "convert by 21:33" on the wall | If past 2 hours: leave it on and get a physician's direction ([02.4](../module-02/lesson-04.md), [02.5](../module-02/lesson-05.md)) |
| Calling Moshe "minor" and not re-checking | He walked in; his pulse stays normal on bisoprolol | Hourly blast checks for 6 hours, written; look at BP, skin, mental state, abdomen ([05.1](../module-05/lesson-01.md)) |
| Ibuprofen for Moshe's "scratches" | Kitchen-drawer reflex, apixaban forgotten | Medication history before any drug ([04.8](../module-04/lesson-08.md)); acetaminophen only |
| Wrapping cling film around the forearm | Habit from bandaging | Strips laid on, not wrapped; check fingers ([05.4](../module-05/lesson-04.md)) |
| A gas stove or candles in the safe room | Cold and the wish to comfort people | Out, now; CO alarm on; layers instead ([06.5](../module-06/lesson-05.md)) |
| No sleep for the leader, a missed dose or turn at 02:00 | Fatigue | Rest in blocks, wake cards, everything written on the wall sheet ([13.2](../module-13/lesson-02.md)) |
| Handover is a long story | No MIST practice | Mechanism, injuries, signs with trend, treatment with times, category: 30 seconds ([01.7](../module-01/lesson-07.md)) |

## Scope and law

Everything in this scenario except the drugs is within reasonable lay first aid or EMT practice: tourniquets, reassessment, packing, burn cooling and dressing, positioning, warming, monitoring, documentation and moving. Tourniquet conversion is taught in TCCC down to non-medic levels but is not an Israeli EMT skill; giving a prescription antibiotic is a physician's decision even when the prescription was for your kit. Do them as the last resort described in [01.5 · The law and your scope in Israel](../module-01/lesson-05.md), ideally under a physician's direction by phone, and write down who directed you and when. The Duty to Rescue Law gives no immunity.

## Practice

> [!WARNING]
> Practise conversion and tourniquets only on a willing adult partner for short periods (under 2 minutes of full tightening, never on anyone with a clotting or vascular problem), or on a model. Never practise needles or drugs on a person.

**What you need:** this lesson printed or on a second screen, three printed casualty cards ([01.7](../module-01/lesson-07.md)), three flowsheets ([07.8](../module-07/lesson-08.md)), a wall sheet on paper, a timer, your home kit or a training kit (a training tourniquet, gauze, a pressure bandage, cling film), a pool noodle or a rolled towel taped to a partner's thigh as a wound model, a jug and a marked bottle, a calculator.

**Solo (about 2 hours):** read each decision point, start the timer, write your decision on the card or flowsheet before the time limit, then open the reference. At T+0:05 and T+1:10, actually apply, tighten, pack and convert on the wound model, timing yourself. At T+6:45 and T+8:00, say the handovers out loud, recorded on your phone, and time them.

**With a partner (about 2.5 hours):** the partner plays all three casualties and reads the situation lines, adding the vital signs only when you check them ("you have not counted his pulse"). The partner may add one surprise card per run, for example "Dana's dressing is red at 23:00" or "Noam vomits again at 01:00", and checks your reaction against the lessons.

**Household-only replay (about 1.5 hours):** a week later, run it again with only household items (the substitution table in Improvise it). Improvise the tourniquet, packing, burn cover and urine measure physically.

### Scoring rubric

Score each point **2** (actions, order and reason match the reference), **1** (right action, wrong order or missing reason) or **0** (missing or wrong action, or over the time limit).

| Decision point | Key items for 2 points |
|---|---|
| T+0:00 Arrival | Four questions; Dana first; Ruth given pressure on the scalp; Moshe not "minor"; room set up |
| T+0:05 Tourniquet | Pulse checked; second TQ on skin 5-7.5 cm above; first loosened; times written; "convert by 21:33" |
| T+0:15 Noam | Scalp pressure; burn sized about 6-8% with palms, under 10%; cooled 20 min; loose cling film; 375 mg / 250 mg (or 200 mg) by weight |
| T+0:25 Moshe | Full survey; hourly blast checks; bisoprolol and apixaban warnings written |
| T+0:40 Call and drugs | Scripted call with one question and read-back; cefadroxil 1 g × 2 adults; no NSAID for Moshe; stock counted |
| T+1:10 Conversion | Three criteria named; under 2 h; pack, 3 min, bandage, slow release, loose TQ left; documented |
| T+2:30 Siren | No stove, no candles, CO alarm, layers |
| T+3:00 Moshe changes | Trend recognised; Urgent; nil by mouth; 15-min checks; emergent call |
| T+4:30 Night plan | Urine targets calculated; Noam neuro checks; turns; rest plan with wake cards |
| T+6:00 Moshe worse | Shock named; no oral fluid; emergent call; packaged to go first |
| T+6:45 and T+8:00 | MIST under 30 s each; correct categories; tourniquet and drugs handed over |

**Maximum 22.** How you know it worked: **18 or more**, no zero on T+0:05, T+1:10 or T+3:00, and your cards and flowsheets alone (without your memory) would let the ambulance crew reconstruct the night. Below 18, reread the lessons linked in the points you lost and run it again in a week with the partner changing two cards.
