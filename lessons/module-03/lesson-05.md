# 03.5 · Finger thoracostomy

By the end of this lesson you will know what a finger thoracostomy is, when the TCCC Guidelines call for one, where and how it is done, and how to prepare and assist when a physician or paramedic performs it. It is an awareness lesson: this is the most invasive chest procedure in the course, and in TCCC it belongs only to the highest tier (Combat Paramedic/Provider).

> [!CAUTION]
> Performing these procedures on people without the required state certification is illegal.
> This lesson is for educational purposes only. Practise only on models and animal tissue.

## Why it matters

A needle decompression can fail: the needle is too short, it kinks, or blood clots in it. A **finger thoracostomy** opens the chest through a short cut in the side, and the operator's finger enters the chest cavity directly. Air (and blood) can escape through a hole far bigger than any needle, and the finger confirms that the operator really is in the chest.

TCCC 2026 places it under **refractory shock**: if a casualty in shock is not responding to fluid resuscitation, consider untreated tension pneumothorax. Chest trauma, persistent respiratory distress, absent breath sounds and SpO2 below 90% support this. Treat with **repeated needle decompression, or finger thoracostomy / chest tube at the 5th intercostal space in the anterior axillary line**, according to the skills, experience and authorisations of the treating provider. TCCC also warns that a finger thoracostomy **may not stay open**, and finger decompression through the incision may have to be repeated.

In practice, physician-led helicopter and trauma teams use it mostly on casualties who are being ventilated with positive pressure (BVM or ventilator), because in a casualty breathing on their own an open hole in the chest wall can behave like an open chest wound. That is one more reason it belongs to someone trained to manage what follows.

## What to do

### Where: the safe triangle

The insertion is on the **side of the chest**, in the "safe triangle":

- **Front edge:** the outer edge of the large chest muscle (pectoralis major), the front fold of the armpit.
- **Back edge:** the front edge of the large back muscle (latissimus dorsi), the back fold of the armpit.
- **Bottom:** a horizontal line at about the level of the nipple in a man (the 5th intercostal space).
- **Top:** the armpit.

The TCCC skill card selects the **4th or 5th intercostal space** at the **anterior axillary line**, or the **5th intercostal space at the midaxillary line**: a line from the armpit to the side of the nipple in men, or about 2-5 cm above the lower end of the breastbone in women. Lower than this risks the liver, spleen and diaphragm.

### Equipment (TCCC skill instruction list)

Sterile gloves, stethoscope, sterile drape, scalpel (#10 or #15 blade), Kelly clamp (a large curved artery forceps), antiseptic (povidone-iodine or chlorhexidine), 1% lidocaine with an 18G needle to draw up and a 23G 1.5-inch needle to inject, a 10 ml syringe, alcohol pads, 4x4 gauze, tape, and a casualty card.

### The procedure (TCCC skill card, Combat Paramedic/Provider)

What the provider does, in order, and what you do to help:

1. **Explain** the procedure to the casualty if conscious. *You: reassure, hold the hand on the other side.*
2. **Record baseline vital signs** and respiratory assessment. *You: take and write them.*
3. **Prepare the casualty:** position, and raise the arm on the affected side above the head; in a woman the breast is moved aside and held for the whole procedure. *You: firmly hold the arm above the head.*
4. **Identify** the safe triangle and the site.
5. **Clean** the site with antiseptic.
6. Put on **sterile gloves**.
7. **Drape** the area.
8. **Local anaesthetic:** draw up 1% lidocaine with the 18G needle; infiltrate generously under the skin and into the space between the ribs with the 23G needle. Keep the total **under 0.5 ml/kg of 1% lidocaine**. If the casualty has had ketamine for sedation or pain, lidocaine may not be needed. *You: open packages onto the drape without touching the inside.*
9. **Incision and entry:**
   - A **2-3 cm skin incision parallel to the rib**, over the selected site or directly over the rib (the rib is a backstop for the blade), down to the muscle between the ribs.
   - With the **Kelly clamp**, **blunt dissection** through the soft tissue, passing **over the top edge of the rib** into the chosen space (the vessels and nerve run under each rib), and puncture the inner lining of the chest (parietal pleura).
   - A **"pop"** is heard and felt as the clamp tips enter the cavity.
   - The clamp is placed with jaws closed on the rib, pointing to the space above, then **spread** to force the tissue apart.
   - Holding the hole open with the clamp, the operator inserts a **gloved finger** through the incision into the chest to **verify position**, then removes the clamp.
   - The finger **widens** the opening and checks there are no **adhesions** (the lung stuck to the chest wall).
   - **Feel for the lung:** air and the **pink, spongy lung** should be immediately inside. If not, the finger may be in the **abdomen** (below the diaphragm).
   - Allow **10-15 seconds** for the air to escape, then remove the finger.
10. **Dressing:** 4x4 gauze with tape as a protective dressing, **not airtight**. *You: tape the dressing.*
11. **Reassess:** breath sounds on both sides, clinical improvement (distress better, SpO2 90% or higher), **vital signs every 15 minutes**, and **pain relief** (Module 4). *You: run the flowsheet.*
12. **Document** on the casualty card.

A **tube thoracostomy** (chest tube) follows the same opening: a 16-36 Fr tube is passed in, connected to a one-way valve, sutured in place and covered with an occlusive dressing. It is a hospital or physician procedure and is not taught in this course.

### Signs it is working, and signs it has closed

- **Working:** rush of air or blood on entry, distress improves, SpO2 rises, radial pulse returns or strengthens.
- **Closed:** the improvement fades; the dressing stays dry when it was wet; distress returns. The opening in the muscle closes as tissue shifts. The provider **re-inserts a gloved finger** through the same incision (re-fingering). Watch for this every few minutes.

## Improvise it

If a qualified provider is present but the kit is not, these are the substitutes and their costs:

| Item | Household substitute | Risk / how it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Scalpel | A new utility-knife or craft-knife blade, or a small sharp clean kitchen knife | A blunt or long blade cuts too deep or tears; keep the cut short and shallow, rib as backstop |
| Kelly clamp | Blunt-tipped scissors inserted closed and opened inside; a large blunt artery forceps from a sewing or fishing kit | Pointed tips puncture the lung; sharp scissors cut vessels. Blunt tips only |
| Sterile gloves | The cleanest gloves available, hands scrubbed with soap first | Infection of the chest cavity (empyema) days later: watch for fever (Module 8) |
| Sterile drape | A freshly ironed or boiled cotton cloth, or clean cling film around the site | Contamination; better than none |
| Antiseptic | Chlorhexidine or povidone from a home kit; 70% rubbing alcohol; soap and water | Spirits under 60% alcohol do not disinfect |
| Lidocaine | None at home; ketamine if prescribed (Module 4) | Severe pain; hold the casualty firmly |
| Non-occlusive dressing | Clean folded gauze or cotton, taped on two sides | An airtight cover turns it into a closed system that can tension again |

Prepare instruments as in lesson 03.4: scrub, then boil 20 minutes or use a pressure cooker, or soak in fresh 0.5% chlorine and rinse with boiled water.

**What does not work:** a ballpoint pen or a tube pushed through the chest wall without opening it; a finger pushed through an incision that has not passed over the top of the rib (it tears the vessels under the rib); and covering the opening with an airtight seal and leaving it.

## When it goes wrong

- **Bleeding** from the incision or the vessels under the rib: firm pressure; it is the reason for staying over the top of the rib.
- **Lung injury** from a sharp instrument pushed too deep: blunt dissection only, never stab.
- **Entering the abdomen** (too low, or during breathing out when the diaphragm rises): the finger feels liver or bowel, not spongy lung. Withdraw and use a higher space.
- **No air, only blood:** a hemothorax; the opening may drain it, but the casualty needs blood and surgery. Treat shock.
- **Closing over:** re-finger through the same incision.
- **Infection** over the following days: fever, pus, worsening breathing (Module 8).
- **Open chest wound effect** in a casualty breathing on their own: watch for worsening distress or air sucking through the hole; the provider decides how to cover it.

## Scope and law

Finger thoracostomy is a physician (and, in some systems, advanced paramedic) procedure. In TCCC it is reserved for the Combat Paramedic/Provider tier, and the Guidelines say it is done "according to the skills, experience, and authorizations of the treating medical provider". As an EMT, your role is to recognise refractory shock with chest signs, prepare the equipment and assist. This course does not suggest that an EMT perform it. See [01.5 · The law and your scope in Israel](../module-01/lesson-05.md).

## Practice

**Drill: equipment layout and assist (40 minutes).** A clean tray or chopping board, a cloth, a blade, blunt scissors or a large artery forceps, gauze, tape, gloves, a syringe, a timer, a lamb or beef rib rack (optional, for the tissue part), a partner.

1. **Landmarks (10 min).** On a partner, mark the safe triangle with a washable marker: front armpit fold, back armpit fold, nipple level. Mark the 5th intercostal space at the anterior and midaxillary lines.
2. **Layout (10 min).** Lay out the equipment in the order the card uses it, from left to right, on a clean cloth: antiseptic, gloves, drape, lidocaine set, blade, clamp, gauze, tape, card. Have your partner call the steps; you hand over each item before it is asked for.
3. **Tissue (15 min, optional).** On the rib rack, with the inner (bone) side up and the meat underneath raised on a bowl, make a 2-3 cm cut parallel to a rib, down to the muscle. Push closed blunt scissors over the top of the lower rib through the inner membrane, open them, and pass a gloved finger through. Feel the edge of the rib above and below your finger.
4. **Card (5 min).** Write a casualty card entry: time, side, site, operator, result, re-finger times.

How you know it worked: your safe triangle marks match the three borders, your tray order matches the card, you handed each item before it was asked for, and on the tissue your finger passed over the top of the lower rib, not under the rib above.
