# 04.7 · Routes and drug math

By the end of this lesson you will be able to pick the best route for a drug in the casualty in front of you, draw it up and give it by mouth, nose, muscle, vein or bone without contaminating it, and calculate any weight-based dose and volume in your head or on paper, including the household version with a crushed tablet and a syringe.

> [!CAUTION]
> Performing these procedures on people without the required state certification is illegal.
> This lesson is for educational purposes only. Practise only on models and animal tissue.

## Why it matters

Most serious medication errors are not about the wrong drug but about the **wrong amount**: a decimal point, a different concentration, mg confused with ml, or an adult habit applied to a child. Under stress, with poor light and gloves on, these errors multiply. The cure is a fixed method you use every time, out loud.

## What to do

### Choose the route

| Route | Onset | Use when | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PO** (swallowed) | 30-60 min | Conscious, can swallow, not in shock, no abdominal wound | Slow and unreliable in shock; vomiting |
| **ODT / buccal** (dissolves on tongue or in cheek) | 10-30 min | Nauseated casualty (ondansetron ODT) | Only drugs made for it |
| **IN** (atomised into the nose) | 5-15 min | No IV; ketamine, esketamine, naloxone | About 0.2-1 ml per nostril; use the highest concentration; blood or mucus blocks absorption |
| **IM** (muscle) | 5-15 min | No IV; ketamine, ceftriaxone, ondansetron, adrenaline | Up to about 1 ml in a small deltoid, 2 ml in an adult deltoid, 3-5 ml in the thigh or gluteal muscle; slower in shock and cold |
| **IV** (vein) | 1-2 min | Access in place; titrating drugs | Needs access; push rates matter |
| **IO** (bone) | As IV | No IV in a casualty needing drugs now | Same doses as IV; flush after every drug (04.2) |
| **PR** (rectal) | 15-60 min | Fluids (04.1), some suppositories | Variable absorption |

### Draw up correctly

**From a vial (rubber stopper):**

1. Check: drug name, **concentration**, expiry, clear liquid, the casualty's allergy.
2. Wipe the stopper with alcohol; let it dry.
3. Draw air into the syringe equal to the volume you need; inject it into the vial (makes drawing easier).
4. Turn the vial upside down, keep the needle tip in the liquid, draw slightly more than needed.
5. Tap out air bubbles, push back to the exact mark at eye level.
6. Change to a fresh needle for injection if you have one (the stopper blunts it).

**From a glass ampoule:**

1. Flick the liquid down from the neck.
2. Wrap the neck in gauze; snap away from you.
3. Draw up with a **filter needle or filter straw** if you have one (glass fragments); otherwise tilt the ampoule and keep the bevel against the side, off the bottom.
4. Change the needle before injecting.

**Label every syringe** at once (tape and marker): drug, total dose, concentration, time. An unlabelled syringe is thrown away.

### Give it

**Intramuscular:**

| Site | Find it | Adult needle | Max volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Outer thigh** (vastus lateralis) | Middle third of the thigh, on the outer front side; divide hip-to-knee into thirds | 23-25G, 25 mm (38 mm in heavy thighs) | 3-5 ml; first choice in children and in shock (TCCC prefers thigh for adrenaline in shock) |
| **Deltoid** | 2-3 finger widths below the bony tip of the shoulder (acromion), in the centre of the muscle | 23-25G, 25 mm | 1-2 ml |
| **Ventrogluteal** | Palm on the greater trochanter, index finger to the front hip bone, middle finger spread back: inject in the V | 21-23G, 38 mm | 3-5 ml |

Steps: expose, clean, stretch the skin flat, needle at **90°** in one quick motion to the hub, inject steadily, withdraw, press with gauze. Aspiration is not needed at these sites. Through clothing in an emergency is acceptable for autoinjectors and in the direct-threat phase; otherwise expose.

**Intranasal (atomiser, e.g. MAD Nasal):**

1. Draw up the dose plus about 0.1 ml for the atomiser's dead space, using the **highest concentration** (TCCC: ketamine 100 mg/ml).
2. Attach the atomiser; head neutral or slightly back.
3. Tip into the nostril, pointing slightly up and out (towards the top of the ear).
4. **Push briskly** to atomise; half the volume in each nostril; aim for 0.2-0.5 ml per side and no more than about 1 ml.
5. Wipe excess; do not let the casualty sniff hard (drug goes to the throat).

**IV push (into a saline lock or running line):**

1. Check the line is in the vein: flush 5 ml saline, no swelling, no pain.
2. Clean the port; connect the drug syringe.
3. Push at the rate the drug requires (ketamine 1 minute, TXA about 1 minute, ondansetron at least 30 seconds, ceftriaxone 5 minutes).
4. **Flush 10 ml saline** (5 ml in small children) so the drug reaches the circulation.
5. Watch the casualty for 5 minutes.

**IO:** exactly as IV, with a brisk flush after each drug and a pressure bag for infusions (04.2).

### The math, every time

**Step 1. Know the weight.**

- Adult: ask, or estimate. A slim average adult is about 70 kg.
- Child, if you cannot weigh (Advanced Paediatric Life Support formulas):

| Age | Estimated weight (kg) |
|---|---|
| 0-12 months | (0.5 × age in months) + 4 |
| 1-5 years | (2 × age in years) + 8 |
| 6-12 years | (3 × age in years) + 7 |

A length-based tape (Broselow) is more accurate when you have one. Weighing on a bathroom scale while holding the child, then subtracting your weight, is better still.

**Step 2. Dose (mg) = dose per kg (mg/kg) × weight (kg).** Check it against the maximum.

**Step 3. Volume (ml) = dose (mg) ÷ concentration (mg/ml).**

**Step 4. Say it out loud** and, if possible, have a second person check: "Ketamine, 20 kilos, one milligram per kilo, twenty milligrams, one hundred milligrams per mil, zero point two mil."

### Concentration language

| On the label | Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| mg/ml | mg in each ml | Ketamine 50 mg/ml |
| **%** | grams per 100 ml; **1% = 10 mg/ml** | Lidocaine 2% = 20 mg/ml; calcium chloride 10% = 100 mg/ml |
| **1:1000** | 1 g in 1000 ml = **1 mg/ml** | Adrenaline 1:1000 |
| mg / 5 ml | Typical for syrups | Paracetamol syrup 120 mg/5 ml = 24 mg/ml |
| mcg | 1/1000 of a mg | Fentanyl 50 mcg/ml |

### Dilution

To make a weaker solution: **C1 × V1 = C2 × V2.**

Example: you want ketamine 5 mg/ml for a slow IV dose from a 50 mg/ml vial. Draw **1 ml** (50 mg) and add **9 ml saline** in a 10 ml syringe: 50 mg in 10 ml = 5 mg/ml. Label "ketamine 5 mg/ml".

Diluting makes small doses measurable and slow pushes easier; it also creates a new concentration that must be labelled.

## Improvise it

### Household measures

- **Kitchen spoons are not measures.** A "teaspoon" from a drawer can hold anywhere from about 2.5 to over 7 ml. For children, that is up to a threefold error.
- **Use an oral syringe** (the one that comes with children's syrups, or any syringe without a needle): it is the most accurate tool in the house. Keep two 5 ml and two 10 ml syringes in every kit.
- If you truly have nothing else: a standard measuring spoon set from baking (5 ml = 1 teaspoon, 15 ml = 1 tablespoon), filled level.

### Splitting and dispersing tablets for a child

1. Use a tablet with a score line if possible; a kitchen knife on a board splits it.
2. For smaller fractions, **crush** the tablet between two spoons, add a known volume of water, and stir: e.g. one 500 mg paracetamol tablet in **10 ml** water = **50 mg/ml**. Draw the dose with a syringe **immediately** after stirring (it settles), give, and discard the rest.
3. **Never crush** extended-release (TCCC's 650 mg acetaminophen caplets), enteric-coated or "retard" tablets: the whole dose is released at once or destroyed by stomach acid.
4. Mix with a little juice or jam if bitter; do not hide it in a whole bottle of milk (the child may not finish it).

### Reusing and sterilising syringes when no new ones exist

A used syringe and needle can carry HIV, hepatitis B and hepatitis C from one person to the next. In order of safety:

1. **New sterile syringe and needle.** Stockpile them; they are cheap.
2. **The same casualty's own syringe**, reused only for that casualty, capped and kept clean.
3. **Steam under pressure:** a kitchen pressure cooker at full pressure (about 121 °C) for at least 20-30 minutes, after washing out all blood, is the nearest thing to sterilisation at home. Glass syringes and metal needles tolerate it; many plastic syringes warp or the plunger seal fails: test by drawing water before use.
4. **Boiling** for at least 20 minutes after cleaning: high-level disinfection, **not sterilisation**. Bacterial spores (including tetanus) survive 100 °C.
5. **Bleach rinse** (the harm-reduction method: water twice, full-strength household bleach twice with 30 seconds of contact, water twice): reduces HIV risk but is unreliable against hepatitis C; CDC states a new sterile syringe is always safer. Last resort only.

Alcohol soaking and wiping do **not** make a syringe safe. A blunted needle tears tissue and hurts; replace needles before syringes if you can only replace one.

### Improvised atomiser and IM tools

- No atomiser: drip the IN dose slowly into the nostril with the casualty's head back (absorption is worse; more runs down the throat).
- No IM needle long enough for a heavy thigh: use the deltoid with the needle you have, or the ventrogluteal site where fat is thinner.

**How you know your measure or dose is failing:** the child is still in pain or still feverish after an hour at what you thought was a full dose (you probably under-dosed), or is unusually drowsy (over-dose). Recalculate with the method above.

## When it goes wrong

- **Wrong concentration.** Ketamine 10, 50 and 100 mg/ml look the same. Read the label, say the concentration out loud.
- **mg and ml confused.** "Give 50" means nothing. Always say both: "50 milligrams, 0.5 ml".
- **Decimal errors.** Write 0.5, never .5; never write 5.0 (read as 50).
- **Adult dose in a child.** Every child dose is per kg, with an adult maximum.
- **Unlabelled syringes.** Two clear syringes on a tray will be swapped. Label or discard.
- **No flush after an IV or IO push.** The drug sits in the line and the next push delivers it all at once.
- **IM into the wrong place.** Too high on the deltoid hits the shoulder joint; the buttock's upper inner quadrant hits the sciatic nerve. Use the landmarks.
- **Air in the syringe for IV.** Expel all air before IV or IO pushes.

## Scope and law

Giving tablets or liquid medicine at label doses is lay or EMT-level aid; adrenaline by autoinjector is within EMT protocol. Injecting prescription drugs by IM, IN, IV or IO route is paramedic or physician practice in Israel, and only your own doctor can prescribe your home supply. The Duty to Rescue Law gives no immunity ([lesson 01.5](../module-01/lesson-05.md)): practise these skills so you can prepare and assist, and inject yourself only as a last resort or under a physician's direction by phone.

## Practice

**Drill: draw, inject, calculate (50 minutes).**

What you need: a raw turkey drumstick or chicken thighs (skin on) or an orange, two vials or small bottles with rubber stoppers filled with water and labelled "ketamine 50 mg/ml" and "ketamine 100 mg/ml", empty glass or plastic ampoules (or a small plastic bottle) labelled "TXA 500 mg/5 ml", 1, 3, 5 and 10 ml syringes, 23G needles, an atomiser if available, tape and a marker, a paracetamol 500 mg tablet, two spoons, a cup, kitchen teaspoons and a measuring spoon set.

1. **Draw-up (10 minutes).** Draw 0.36 ml from the "50 mg/ml" vial (18 mg). Then draw 1 ml and dilute to 10 ml with water; label "5 mg/ml". Draw 20 ml "TXA" from four "ampoules". Label everything.
2. **IM (10 minutes).** On the turkey leg or chicken thigh, give five injections of 1 ml coloured water at 90° to the hub. Cut through afterwards: is the colour in the muscle or in the fat?
3. **IN (5 minutes).** "Give" 0.5 ml with the atomiser into a paper towel tube, 0.25 ml per side; then into a tissue to see the spray pattern.
4. **Spoons (5 minutes).** Fill three different kitchen teaspoons with water and measure each with a syringe. Write the range.
5. **Tablet (5 minutes).** Crush a 500 mg paracetamol tablet into 10 ml of water; draw the dose for a 13 kg child (15 mg/kg).
6. **Weights (5 minutes).** Estimate the weight of a 6-month-old, a 3-year-old, a 7-year-old and a 10-year-old.
7. **Math check (10 minutes).** Calculate: (a) lidocaine 2% for IO, 40 mg: how many ml? (b) adrenaline 1:1000, 0.3 mg: how many ml? (c) calcium chloride 10%, 20 mg/kg for a 15 kg child: ml? (d) ketamine 0.3 mg/kg IV for a 60 kg adult from the 5 mg/ml dilution: ml?

How you know it worked: every syringe is labelled and accurate to the mark; at least four of five IM injections are in muscle; your spoons varied (most people find 3-7 ml); tablet dose 195 mg = 3.9 ml; weights 7, 14, 28 and 37 kg; math (a) 2 ml, (b) 0.3 ml, (c) 300 mg = 3 ml, (d) 18 mg = 3.6 ml.
