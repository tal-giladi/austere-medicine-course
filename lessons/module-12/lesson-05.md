# 12.5 · Dental emergencies: knocked-out tooth, lost filling, abscess

By the end of this lesson you will be able to save a knocked-out adult tooth by putting it back within minutes, cover a broken tooth or a lost filling so it stops hurting, control severe toothache, and manage a dental abscess when no dentist is available, including the swellings that can close the airway.

> [!CAUTION]
> Performing these procedures on people without the required state certification is illegal.
> This lesson is for educational purposes only. Practise only on models and animal tissue.

## Why it matters

Dental problems are among the most common reasons people seek care after any disaster, and in a long disruption there is no dentist. Toothache stops people sleeping, eating and working; an untreated dental abscess can spread into the neck and kill (Ludwig's angina); and a knocked-out front tooth has a good future **only if it is back in its socket within minutes**. Every one of these is something you can act on with a little knowledge and household materials.

## What to do

### 1. Knocked-out (avulsed) tooth

**First: is it a permanent tooth?** Children lose their front baby (primary) teeth from about 6-7 years. A knocked-out tooth in a child **under about 6** is almost certainly primary. **Never replant a primary tooth** (IADT 2020): it can damage the permanent tooth growing underneath. If unsure, compare its size with its neighbours, look for the gap, and ask the parent.

**Replant a permanent tooth at once (IADT 2020 first aid):**

1. **Keep calm**, check for other injuries (head injury, lost consciousness, inhaled tooth: a missing tooth that is not found and a cough means it may be in the lungs).
2. **Find the tooth.** Hold it by the **crown** (the white part), **never by the root**. The living cells on the root decide whether the tooth survives.
3. **If it is dirty**, rinse it **briefly (about 10 seconds)** in milk, saline or the patient's saliva; under cold running water only if there is nothing else. **Do not scrub**, scrape, dry or disinfect it.
4. **Put it back** in its socket, the right way round (compare with the tooth on the other side). Push gently with a thumb until it is level with its neighbours. Bleeding from the socket does not matter: a clot is pushed out.
5. **Hold it in place:** the patient bites gently on a folded handkerchief or gauze.
6. **Note the time** of the injury and of replantation.

**If you cannot replant it** (patient uncooperative, other injuries, unsure which tooth), store it **wet** at once. Storage options in order (IADT 2020 and dental trauma teaching): **Hank's balanced salt solution** (tooth-rescue kits) > **cold milk** > **saliva** (in a cup the patient spits into, or held inside the cheek of an alert adult who will not swallow it) > **saline**. **Water** is a poor medium (it swells and kills root cells) but still better than dry.

**Time is the prognosis:** root cells are most likely to survive if the tooth is replanted within about **15 minutes** or kept in a good medium. After **60 minutes dry**, they are likely dead. IADT still recommends replanting a permanent tooth after a long dry time: it keeps bone and the space, even if it later fuses to the bone.

**After replantation (dentist's job, which you may have to do):**

| Step | IADT 2020 | Field version |
|---|---|---|
| Splint | Flexible, passive splint to the neighbouring teeth for **2 weeks** (4 weeks if the bone is broken) | Improvised splint (Improvise it); soft diet |
| Antibiotics | At the clinician's discretion; evidence limited | Physician decision: commonly amoxicillin; doxycycline in older children and adults (course reading of IADT; verify) |
| Tetanus | Check status; booster if needed for a contaminated injury | Record and refer |
| Care | Soft diet up to 2 weeks; soft toothbrush after every meal; chlorhexidine 0.12% mouthwash twice daily for 1 week | Same; salt-water rinses if no chlorhexidine |
| Root canal | Within 2 weeks for a tooth with a fully formed root | Get to a dentist as soon as one exists |

**Knocked-out primary tooth:** do not replant. Control bleeding by biting on gauze, soft diet, clean the area, and look for injury to the lips and gums.

### 2. Broken tooth

| Type | What you see | Risk | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Enamel only** | Chipped edge, no yellow, no pain | Cosmetic | Smooth a sharp edge with a clean nail file; dental wax over it |
| **Enamel and dentine** | Yellow layer showing, sensitive to cold and air | Bacteria reach the nerve through dentine | **Cover** the dentine within a day (temporary filling, glass ionomer, or the household options below). Keep the fragment in water or milk: a dentist can bond it back |
| **Pulp exposed** | **Pink or red dot** or bleeding from the centre of the break | Nerve infection, abscess | Cover with a temporary filling; pain relief; dentist soon. If a dentist is weeks away, watch for abscess |
| **Root or crown-root fracture** | Part of the tooth moves, bleeding from the gum | Painful, may be swallowed or inhaled | If the loose fragment is attached only by gum and is a choking risk, remove it; otherwise splint like an avulsed tooth |
| **Loosened or displaced tooth** | Tooth wobbly or pushed out of line | Nerve death, bite problems | Gently push back into line within the first hours if it blocks the bite, splint, soft diet |

### 3. Lost filling or crown

1. Clean the cavity: brush gently, rinse with warm salt water, dry it with a twist of tissue.
2. **Temporary filling material** from a pharmacy (zinc oxide-eugenol or similar ready-mixed pastes: Dentemp, Cavit-type products): roll a small ball, press it in, bite gently to shape it, scrape away excess. It lasts days to a few weeks; replace it if it washes out.
3. **Lost crown:** clean the inside of the crown and the tooth, try it in without cement to check the fit, then refix it with **temporary dental cement** or, as a stop-gap, a smear of **denture adhesive** or petroleum jelly. Do **not** use superglue or household glues (they irritate or kill the nerve, may set the crown in the wrong position, and make proper refitting harder; course reasoning from manufacturer warnings). If the crown cannot be refitted, keep it in a labelled bag.

### 4. Toothache

- **Reversible pulpitis:** sharp pain with cold or sweet that stops within seconds. Usually a cavity or a lost filling: cover it.
- **Irreversible pulpitis:** spontaneous, throbbing pain that lingers, wakes the patient, often worse lying down or with heat. The nerve is dying; definitive treatment is root canal or extraction.
- **Abscess:** constant pain, the tooth is tender to tap and feels "high", swelling of the gum or face, sometimes a pimple on the gum that discharges pus.

**Pain relief (ADA 2024 guideline for adolescents and adults):** an **NSAID with acetaminophen** is first line and works better than opioids for dental pain.

| Drug | Adult | Child | Repeat | Max | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ibuprofen + acetaminophen together** | **400 mg + 1000 mg** | 10 mg/kg + 15 mg/kg | Every 6 h | Ibuprofen 1.2 g a day OTC (2.4 g with a physician); acetaminophen 4 g a day; child limits as in 12.1 | ADA 2024 preferred regimen. NSAID cautions as in 04.5 |
| **Naproxen** (alternative NSAID) | 500 mg, then 250 mg | Not in this course | Every 6-8 h | 1,250 mg on day 1, then 1,000 mg a day | Not with ibuprofen |
| **Clove oil** (topical) | 1-2 drops on a cotton pellet held against the tooth | Not under 2 years; one drop only | Up to 3-4 times a day | | See Improvise it |

**Antibiotics do not treat toothache** from pulpitis without swelling or fever; the ADA (2019) recommends against them, because the infection inside the tooth cannot be reached by blood.

### 5. Dental abscess

**Localised abscess** (swelling confined to the gum by the tooth, no fever, no spreading): the treatment is **drainage** (MSF: "treatment is only surgical"). Dentists open the tooth, drain through the gum, or extract it.

1. **Warm salt-water mouth rinses** (1/2 teaspoon salt in a cup of warm water) held over the area for a minute, every 1-2 hours, encourage it to point and drain.
2. A **fluctuant** (soft, fluid-filled) swelling on the gum that points into the mouth may be drained with a small stab incision at its softest point, after local anaesthetic (09.3), using the abscess principles of [09.5 · Abscess drainage](../module-09/lesson-05.md). Cut **parallel to the gum line**, only through the soft point, never deep near the back of the lower jaw (nerve) or the floor of the mouth. Let it drain, rinse with warm salt water. **This is a dentist's or physician's procedure.**
3. **Antibiotics** when there is spreading swelling, fever, or no one can drain it soon (ADA 2019 allows antibiotics when definitive dental care is not immediately available; MSF gives them for 5 days after drainage):

| Drug | Adult | Child | Duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Amoxicillin** | **1 g twice daily** (MSF) or 500 mg three times daily (ADA) | **25 mg/kg twice daily** (MSF) | 5 days (ADA 3-7 days) | First line |
| **Phenoxymethylpenicillin** (penicillin V) | 500 mg four times daily | Physician | 3-7 days | Alternative |
| **Add metronidazole** if not improving at 48-72 h or severe | 500 mg three times daily (ADA) or 400 mg three times daily (UK) | 7.5 mg/kg three times daily (physician) | 5-7 days | No alcohol |
| **Penicillin allergy** | **Clindamycin 300 mg four times daily**, or azithromycin 500 mg day 1 then 250 mg days 2-5 (ADA 2019) | Clindamycin 10 mg/kg three times daily | 3-7 days | |
| **Spreading facial infection** | **Ceftriaxone 2 g IV/IM** once daily **plus metronidazole** 500 mg every 8 h (or amoxicillin-clavulanate if only oral is possible) | Ceftriaxone 50-75 mg/kg, metronidazole as above | Until a surgeon takes over | Evacuate: course plan from 08.5 and 04.6 |

> [!WARNING]
> **Emergency signs (spreading deep neck infection, Ludwig's angina):** swelling under the jaw or of the floor of the mouth, the tongue pushed up or forward, drooling, difficulty swallowing or speaking, unable to open the mouth (trismus), noisy breathing, fever, swelling spreading to the eye or down the neck. Sit the patient **upright and leaning forward**, never lay them flat, give the first antibiotic dose, and **evacuate urgently**: the airway can close within hours and needs a surgeon (a surgical airway, 03.6, is the last resort).

**Extraction** is outside this course. A tooth hanging only by gum, or so loose it may be inhaled, can be removed with gauze-covered fingers; anything more needs a dentist.

## Improvise it

| Need | Household version | What works | What fails / the risk | How to tell it is failing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tooth storage** | Cold milk (any; long-life is fine); saliva in a cup; saline (1/2 teaspoon salt in 250 ml boiled water); cling film wrap with a little saliva if nothing liquid | Keeps root cells alive for some time | Water: swells cells; dry tissue: cells die in about an hour | Delay: replant as soon as possible |
| **Splint for a replanted tooth** | A strip of **aluminium foil** folded several times, moulded over the front and back of the tooth and one neighbour each side, the patient biting gently to hold it; or **sugar-free chewing gum** pressed over the tooth and neighbours | Holds the tooth while the gum settles | Foil can be swallowed or cut the gum; gum softens. Replace daily; soft diet. Rigid fixing (wire, superglue on teeth) can harm the root | Tooth moves more each day, or pain and swelling rise (infection) |
| **Temporary filling** | **Sugar-free** gum, chewed soft and pressed in; or plain candle **paraffin wax** softened in warm hands; dental or orthodontic wax | Covers sensitive dentine for a day or two | Sugared gum feeds decay; coloured or scented candles contain additives; nothing household seals as well as zinc oxide-eugenol | Pain returns, the filling washes out |
| **Clove oil** (eugenol) | A few whole **cloves** chewed gently next to the tooth, or 1-2 drops of clove essential oil diluted in a teaspoon of olive oil on a cotton bud | Eugenol numbs exposed dentine and pulp for an hour or two | Undiluted oil burns the gum; repeated large amounts are toxic, and **clove oil swallowed by small children can cause liver damage and seizures** (keep out of reach) | Burning, white patches on the gum: stop |
| **Salt-water rinse** | 1/2 teaspoon salt in a cup of warm (not hot) water | Comfort; cleans; helps an abscess point | Does not cure infection | Swelling spreads |
| **Cold** | Ice wrapped in a cloth on the cheek, 15 minutes on, 15 off | Reduces swelling and pain after trauma | **Heat** on the outside of the face can draw an abscess outward to the skin | |
| **Mouthwash** | Chlorhexidine if available; otherwise salt water | | Alcohol mouthwashes sting open tissue | |

**Improvised antiseptic for the mouth:** no bleach, no hydrogen peroxide above 1.5%, no spirits held in the mouth (they burn and do not disinfect a cavity).

## When it goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | Recovery |
|---|---|---|
| Replanting a baby tooth | Damage to the permanent tooth | Never replant primary teeth |
| Holding or scrubbing the root | Dead root cells, tooth fuses to bone | Crown only; brief rinse; no scrubbing |
| Storing the tooth dry in a tissue | Root cells dead within an hour | Milk, saliva, saline; replant fast |
| Tooth replanted back to front | Poor fit, will not stay | Compare with the other side; remove and turn |
| Superglue on a crown or between teeth | Nerve damage, wrong position | Temporary cement or denture adhesive; dentist |
| Antibiotics for every toothache | No benefit, wasted stock | Pain relief and covering the tooth; antibiotics for swelling and fever |
| Abscess treated with antibiotics only, no drainage | Recurs or spreads | Drain; extraction or root canal when a dentist exists |
| Heat packs on a swollen face | Abscess points through the skin | Cold outside, warm salt water inside |
| Laying a patient with floor-of-mouth swelling flat | Airway obstruction | Upright, leaning forward; evacuate |
| Clove oil left within reach of a toddler | Poisoning | Lock it away |

## Scope and law

Replanting a knocked-out tooth, storing it, covering a broken tooth with over-the-counter temporary filling, salt-water rinses and over-the-counter pain relief are first aid. Incising an abscess, local anaesthetic injection (09.3), splinting by bonding, prescribing antibiotics and extracting teeth are dentist or physician powers in Israel; do them only under remote professional direction or as the last resort described in [01.5 · The law and your scope in Israel](../module-01/lesson-05.md), and record what you did for the dentist who follows.

## Practice

**Drill 1: replantation on a model (15 minutes).** You need a raw chicken thigh or drumstick (the meat stands in for gum; kosher butcher), a small piece of carrot or a clean bone fragment shaped like a tooth with a "crown" and a "root", a cup of milk, a timer.

1. Push the "tooth" into the meat to make a socket, pull it out, and drop it on a clean surface.
2. Start the timer. Pick it up **by the crown only**, rinse it 10 seconds in milk, check which way round it goes, replant it level, and "bite" on folded gauze.
3. Repeat 5 times. Target: under 60 seconds from pickup to holding gauze, never touching the root.

**Drill 2: temporary filling and foil splint (20 minutes).** A pharmacy temporary filling kit (or sugar-free gum), aluminium foil, a partner or your own mouth, a mirror, clean hands.

1. On your own back tooth (a groove on the biting surface), place a pea-sized ball of temporary filling, bite to shape it, and scrape away excess with a clean fingernail. Remove it afterwards with a toothpick.
2. Fold foil into a strip 1 cm wide, 4 layers thick; mould it over your own front tooth and its neighbours, front and back. Check it does not cut the gum. Remove.

**Drill 3: dental triage (10 minutes, paper).** Decide primary or permanent, the first action and drug doses for:

1. A 5-year-old knocks out an upper front tooth on a slide.
2. A 14-year-old knocks out an upper front tooth; it fell on the pavement 20 minutes ago.
3. A 40-year-old (70 kg) with a throbbing tooth, swollen gum, no fever, no dentist for two weeks.
4. A 35-year-old with toothache, swelling under the jaw spreading to the neck, drooling, fever.

<details>
<summary>Reference answers</summary>

1. Primary tooth: do not replant. Bite on gauze, soft diet, check lips and gums.
2. Permanent: rinse 10 s in milk or saline holding the crown, replant at once, bite on gauze, foil or gum splint, soft diet 2 weeks, tetanus check, physician for antibiotics, dentist when possible.
3. Localised abscess: warm salt-water rinses, ibuprofen 400 mg + acetaminophen 1 g every 6 h; because no dentist can drain it soon, amoxicillin 1 g twice daily for 5 days (physician); drainage of a pointing swelling by a professional; watch for spreading.
4. Spreading deep neck infection (Ludwig's): upright, ceftriaxone 2 g plus metronidazole 500 mg, urgent evacuation, airway watch.

</details>

How you know it worked: you replant the model tooth in under a minute without touching the root, your temporary filling stays in for an hour of normal talking, and your triage decisions match the box.
