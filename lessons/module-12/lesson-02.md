# 12.2 · Common infections: chest, urine, skin, ear, throat and gut

By the end of this lesson you will be able to recognise the six infections that make up most of the antibiotic decisions in a cut-off community, decide which ones actually need an antibiotic, and give the right drug at the full adult and child dose for the right number of days, with a safe choice for allergy and pregnancy. You will also know the complications that turn each of them into an emergency.

> [!CAUTION]
> Performing these procedures on people without the required state certification is illegal.
> This lesson is for educational purposes only. Practise only on models and animal tissue.

## Why it matters

In a long disruption, the antibiotics in a house or a community are a fixed, small stock. Most coughs, sore throats, earaches and diarrhoeas are viral and get better without them. A few bacterial infections (pneumonia, a kidney infection, spreading cellulitis, strep throat, dysentery) get dangerous without them. Using the stock well means **treating the right patients fully and the wrong ones not at all**: a half course that runs out on day 2 helps nobody.

First decide whether the patient is dangerous ([12.1 · Fever and infection: who is dangerous](lesson-01.md)). Red features mean ceftriaxone and evacuation, whatever the source. This lesson is for the patient who is ill but not in danger, and for finding the source.

## What to do

### General rules (course rules drawn from MSF and NICE)

1. **Find the source** before choosing a drug: ask, look, count, and examine chest, throat, ears, skin (all of it), abdomen and urine.
2. **Weigh the child**, or estimate (04.7). Write every dose as mg/kg and the total.
3. **Full dose, full course, on time.** The durations below are the current short courses (5 days for most chest and ear infections); do not shorten them further.
4. **Ask about allergy** (04.6): rash years ago is not anaphylaxis.
5. **Reassess at 48-72 hours.** No better: check the dose was taken, look for a complication, think of a different source. Worse at any time: 12.1 red pathway.
6. **Record** drug, dose, times and tablets left, so the stock is known.

### Common antibiotic doses used in this lesson

| Drug | Adult | Child | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|

| **Amoxicillin-clavulanate** (dose as amoxicillin) | 875/125 mg twice daily (or 500/62.5 mg two tablets twice daily) | 25 mg/kg twice daily (under 40 kg) | Diarrhoea common |

| **Clindamycin** | 600 mg three times daily (MSF skin) | 10 mg/kg three times daily | Penicillin allergy; diarrhoea, rarely C. difficile |
| **Doxycycline** | 200 mg on day 1, then 100 mg daily | Not under 8 years (US) or 12 years (UK); then 2 mg/kg twice on day 1 then once daily (max as adult) | Take upright with water; sun sensitivity; not in pregnancy |

### Chest

**Recognise.** Cough, fever, fast breathing, sometimes chest pain worse on breathing in, and crackles or reduced breath sounds on one side. In children under 5, **fast breathing for age is the key sign** (12.1: 50 or more at 2-11 months, 40 or more at 1-5 years). **Acute bronchitis** (cough, normal breathing rate, no chest signs, well) is usually viral: no antibiotic, cough may last 3 weeks.

**Severe (MSF):** in adults breathing over 30, heart rate over 125, confusion, blue lips, indrawing; in children chest indrawing, grunting, SpO2 under 90%, unable to drink, any child under 2 months. Severe means ceftriaxone and evacuation.

| Patient | First line | Penicillin allergy | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Child 2 months-5 years, not severe | **Amoxicillin 30 mg/kg three times daily** | Clarithromycin 7.5 mg/kg twice daily (physician) | 5 days |
| Adult and child over 5, not severe | **Amoxicillin 1 g three times daily** (child 30 mg/kg, max 3 g a day) | **Doxycycline** 200 mg day 1 then 100 mg daily, or **clarithromycin** 500 mg twice daily (NICE NG138) | 5 days |
| Not better after 3 days of correct treatment | Add **azithromycin** or clarithromycin (atypical bacteria, MSF) | | |
| Severe, waiting for evacuation | **Ceftriaxone** IM/IV: adult 1 g once daily (MSF; 2 g if sepsis, 08.5), child 50 mg/kg once daily | | At least 3 days, then oral amoxicillin to complete 7-10 |

**COPD flare** (more breathless, more sputum, sputum turns green or yellow): the antibiotic choice is the same as for pneumonia (amoxicillin or doxycycline for 5 days); add prednisolone 40 mg daily for 5 days if the patient has it (12.3).

**Complications:** empyema (fever that returns or persists, one-sided dull chest), lung abscess, sepsis. All need a physician.

### Urine

**Recognise.** Burning, frequency, urgency, lower belly pain, cloudy or smelly urine. A **urine dipstick** helps: nitrite and leucocytes both positive makes infection likely; both negative makes it unlikely in a young woman. In elderly people, a dipstick is unreliable (many have bacteria in the urine without infection): treat symptoms, not strips.

**Kidney infection (pyelonephritis)** is cystitis **plus fever over 38 °C and flank (loin) pain** or tenderness, often vomiting. It can become sepsis within a day.

| Patient | First line | Alternative | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Woman, not pregnant, bladder only | **Nitrofurantoin 100 mg three times daily** (MSF) or **fosfomycin 3 g** single dose | Cefixime 200 mg twice daily | Nitrofurantoin 5 days; cefixime 5 days |
| Pregnant or breastfeeding, bladder only | **Fosfomycin 3 g** single dose | Nitrofurantoin 7 days (not in the last month); cefixime 5 days | Physician should confirm in pregnancy |
| Man with urinary symptoms | Nitrofurantoin or cefixime | | **7 days** (NICE); fever or perineal pain may be prostatitis: physician |
| Girl over 2 years, bladder only | **Cefixime 8 mg/kg once daily** or amoxicillin-clavulanate 12.5 mg/kg twice daily (MSF) | | 3 days |
| Kidney infection, adult, able to swallow | **Ceftriaxone 1 g IM** once, then **ciprofloxacin 500 mg twice daily** (MSF) | Amoxicillin-clavulanate 875/125 mg twice daily or cefixime 200 mg twice daily | Ciprofloxacin 7 days; others 10-14 days |
| Kidney infection, child over 1 month | **Ceftriaxone 50 mg/kg** once daily until better (at least 3 days, physician) | Then amoxicillin-clavulanate 25 mg/kg twice daily | 10 days total |

> [!WARNING]
> **Requires physician confirmation:** urinary infection in a child under 2 years, a man with fever, any pregnant woman with fever or loin pain, and anyone with a urinary catheter. Nitrofurantoin and fosfomycin do **not** treat kidney infection.

### Skin

**Cellulitis and erysipelas.** A spreading, warm, red, tender, swollen area, often on a leg, often from a crack between the toes, a bite or a scratch. Erysipelas has a sharp raised edge. **Mark the edge** with a pen and write the date and time (08.5).

| Patient | First line | Penicillin allergy | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adult or child 12 and over | **Cefalexin 1 g twice daily** or amoxicillin-clavulanate 875/125 mg twice daily (MSF) | **Clindamycin 600 mg three times daily** | 7-10 days (NICE allows 5-7 if improving) |
| Child 1 month-12 years | **Cefalexin 25 mg/kg twice daily** or amoxicillin-clavulanate 25 mg/kg twice daily | Clindamycin 10 mg/kg three times daily | 7-10 days |
| Fever, spreading fast, or not better at 48 h | Ceftriaxone IM/IV (adult 2 g, child 50 mg/kg) and a physician | | |

- **Elevate** the limb (above the heart); treat athlete's foot between the toes; the redness may look worse on day 1 before it improves.
- **A pus collection** (fluctuant, pointing) needs drainage more than antibiotics: [09.5 · Abscess drainage](../module-09/lesson-05.md).
- **Impetigo** (honey-coloured crusts, children): wash crusts off with soap and water; a few spots need only hygiene; widespread needs cefalexin or amoxicillin-clavulanate for 5-7 days.
- **Danger:** pain out of proportion, rapid spread, dusky or blistered skin, crackling (necrotising infection, 08.5). Cellulitis around the eye with swelling of the lids, pain on moving the eye or reduced vision (orbital cellulitis) is an emergency: ceftriaxone and evacuation.

### Ear

**Acute otitis media.** Ear pain (in babies crying, pulling the ear, not sleeping), fever, sometimes pus from the ear after the drum bursts (often with sudden relief of pain). With an otoscope: a red **bulging** drum or pus. A red drum alone is often viral.

MSF: **antibiotics at once** for children **under 2 years**, those with severe signs (vomiting, fever over 39 °C, severe pain), those at risk (malnutrition, immune problems), or when you cannot re-examine within 48-72 hours. Otherwise give painkillers and re-examine in 48-72 hours; most settle without antibiotics.

| Patient | First line | Failure after 48 h | Penicillin allergy | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Child | **Amoxicillin 30 mg/kg three times daily** (max 3 g a day) | Amoxicillin-clavulanate 25 mg/kg twice daily | Azithromycin 10 mg/kg once daily for 3 days (over 6 months) | 5 days |
| Adult | **Amoxicillin 1 g three times daily** | Amoxicillin-clavulanate 875/125 mg twice daily | Azithromycin 500 mg once daily for 3 days | 5 days |

- Pus from the ear: **dry mop** the canal gently with a twist of clean tissue several times a day; no drops (MSF), no syringing.
- **Otitis externa** (pain when the ear lobe is pulled, itchy swollen canal, often after swimming): no oral antibiotic unless it spreads to the face; keep the ear dry; acidifying or antibiotic drops if available.
- **Danger:** swelling, redness or tenderness **behind** the ear with the ear pushed forward (mastoiditis), facial weakness, severe headache or drowsiness: ceftriaxone and evacuation.

### Throat

Most sore throats are viral. MSF uses the **Centor score**, 1 point each: temperature over 38 °C; **no cough**; tender swollen glands at the front of the neck; swollen tonsils or pus on them. **2 or more points:** treat for group A strep (to prevent rheumatic fever and abscess). **1 or less:** viral, no antibiotic. Patients with past rheumatic fever: always treat.

| Drug | Adult | Child | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phenoxymethylpenicillin** (penicillin V) | 1 g twice daily (12 and over) | Under 1 year 125 mg; 1-5 years 250 mg; 6-11 years 500 mg, all twice daily | 10 days |
| **Amoxicillin** (MSF alternative) | 1 g twice daily | 25 mg/kg twice daily | 6 days |
| **Benzathine benzylpenicillin** IM | 1.2 million IU once (30 kg and over) | 600,000 IU once (under 30 kg) | Single dose |
| **Azithromycin** (penicillin allergy) | 500 mg once daily | 20 mg/kg once daily (max 500 mg) | 3 days |

**Danger signs (MSF):** severe pain on one side, unable to swallow saliva, muffled "hot potato" voice, unable to open the mouth (trismus), uvula pushed to one side: **peritonsillar abscess**, needs drainage. A child sitting forward, drooling, quiet, with stridor: **epiglottitis**. Do not look in the throat; keep the child calm on the parent's lap and evacuate (ceftriaxone if evacuation is delayed).

### Gut

Most diarrhoea and vomiting is viral or toxin-related: **oral rehydration is the treatment** ([lesson 11.3](../module-11/lesson-03.md)), with zinc for children. Antibiotics are for:

| Situation | First line (MSF) | Alternative | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dysentery** (visible blood in stool, fever, cramps) | **Ciprofloxacin** 500 mg twice daily; child 15 mg/kg twice daily (max 1 g a day) | **Azithromycin** day 1: adult 500 mg, child 12 mg/kg; days 2-5: adult 250 mg, child 6 mg/kg | Cipro 3 days; azithro 5 days |
| Suspected cholera outbreak | Physician or outbreak team decides; rehydration first | | |

**Danger:** signs of severe dehydration, a hard swollen tender belly, green vomit, blood in stool in a child who is very ill, pain that started in the middle and moved to the lower right (appendicitis): physician and evacuation.

## Improvise it

| Need | Household version | What works | What fails / the risk | How to tell it is failing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Easier breathing in a chest infection** | Sit up, lean forward on a table with pillows; prop babies at 30 degrees; clear the nose with saline drops (1/4 tsp salt in 250 ml boiled, cooled water) and a bulb or by gentle suction | Upright posture lets the diaphragm work; a clear nose helps babies feed | Lying flat makes breathing harder | Faster breathing, indrawing, blue lips: 12.1 red |
| **Steam** | Sitting in a steamy bathroom (shower running) | Comfort for croup and blocked noses; evidence of real benefit is weak | **Bowls of boiling water scald children** every year: never put a child's face over a bowl | Burns; no improvement in breathing within 20-30 minutes |
| **Honey for cough** | 1/2-1 tsp honey before bed, children **over 1 year** only | Modest cough relief (Cochrane) | **Never under 1 year** (infant botulism) | Persistent fast breathing means pneumonia, not cough |
| **Ear pain** | Paracetamol or ibuprofen (12.1 doses); a warm (not hot) cloth over the ear | Most of the benefit in otitis media is pain relief | Oil, garlic or anything else in the ear: risk if the drum has burst | Swelling behind the ear, discharge with fever |
| **Swimmer's ear drops** | Equal parts white vinegar (5%) and boiled water (about 2.5% acetic acid), 3-4 drops three times daily, **only with an intact eardrum** (course reasoning from acetic acid drops) | Acidifies the canal | Stings; harmful if the drum is perforated (recent discharge, ear surgery, grommets) | Spreading redness to the face, fever |
| **Cystitis comfort** | Drink more water; paracetamol | Comfort | Cranberry juice does not treat an infection | Fever or loin pain: kidney infection |
| **Cellulitis edge** | Ballpoint pen line with date and time; phone photo | Objective trend | Washable pen | Line crossed after 24 h of antibiotics |
| **Weighing a child** | Bathroom scale: weigh adult holding child, subtract the adult | Accurate within 0.5 kg | Guessing | Dose errors |

**Antibiotics from a cabinet.** Leftover amoxicillin is a good drug for chest, ear and throat infections and is useless for most skin infections. Leftover ciprofloxacin is good for kidney infection and dysentery and poor for chest and skin. Match the drug to the infection with the tables above, count tablets before starting (a course you cannot finish is a dose for the most serious patient instead), and check expiry (12.4).

## When it goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | Recovery |
|---|---|---|
| Antibiotic for a viral cold, bronchitis or sore throat with Centor 0-1 | Stock wasted, side effects | No antibiotic; safety-net and recheck |
| Counting breathing for 15 seconds in a coughing child | Pneumonia missed | Full minute, calm child |
| Nitrofurantoin for fever and loin pain | Kidney infection untreated, sepsis | Ceftriaxone then ciprofloxacin or cefixime |
| Under-dosing a child (adult tablet halved by guess) | Treatment failure | mg/kg every time (04.7) |
| Stopping when better on day 2 | Relapse | Complete the course in the table |
| Amoxicillin for cellulitis | Staph often resistant | Cefalexin or amoxicillin-clavulanate |
| Amoxicillin for a sore throat that is glandular fever | Widespread itchy rash (not true allergy) | Stop; record "rash with EBV", not "penicillin allergy" |
| Looking down the throat of a drooling child with stridor | Complete airway obstruction | Keep calm, upright, evacuate |
| Antibiotic for watery diarrhoea | No benefit, side effects | ORS and zinc ([lesson 11.3](../module-11/lesson-03.md)) |

## Scope and law

Assessing, examining, giving fluids and over-the-counter pain relief, and calling a physician are within EMT and lay first aid. Every antibiotic in this lesson is a prescription drug in Israel; a physician must prescribe it, by phone or telemedicine if that is all there is. Starting one yourself is the last resort described in [01.5 · The law and your scope in Israel](../module-01/lesson-05.md), justified only when the infection is dangerous, no physician can be reached and waiting would cost the patient's life or health. Keep a record of each dose for the physician who takes over.

1. Pneumonia (fast breathing for age). Amoxicillin 30 mg/kg = 480 mg (9.6 ml of 250 mg/5 ml, round to 10 ml) three times daily for 5 days. Danger: indrawing, grunting, not drinking, drowsy.

6. Dysentery. Ciprofloxacin 500 mg twice daily for 3 days; ORS. Danger: dehydration signs, severe belly pain.
7. Centor 0-1, viral. No antibiotic; fluids, paracetamol, safety-net.
