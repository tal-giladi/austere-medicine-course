# 07.4 · Nursing care: turning, skin, mouth, toilet and food

By the end of this lesson you will be able to run the nursing care that keeps a bed-bound casualty from developing pressure sores, pneumonia, blood clots, infections and malnutrition over 72 hours: turning and padding, mouth care, washing with little water, urine and bowel care without a catheter, and feeding. None of it needs a licence or a hospital, and all of it can be taught to the people around you.

> [!CAUTION]
> Performing these procedures on people without the required state certification is illegal.
> This lesson is for educational purposes only. Practise only on models and animal tissue.

## Why it matters

A casualty who cannot move is under attack from their own bed. Skin over the sacrum, heels and back of the head starts to die when it is pressed against a hard surface for hours. A dry, dirty mouth breeds the bacteria that cause pneumonia. Still legs grow clots. A wet groin breaks down. A casualty who does not eat or drink for three days heals slowly and fights infection badly. The JTS 2025 guideline calls nursing, wound care and splint care possibly the most important yet overlooked capabilities in prolonged care, because they prevent complications without costly equipment and can be done by non-medical team members.

## What to do

### The nursing care plan

Write this schedule on the flowsheet (or a separate card) at the start of the hold, with times and who does what. Intervals are from the JTS 2025 guideline.

| Task | Interval | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Reposition and check padding | Every **2 h** (every **1 h** on a hard surface) | Any trained helper (two people) |
| Check for incontinence (unconscious) | Every **2 h** | Any helper |
| Oral care | Conscious: brush every **12 h**. Unconscious: every **4 h** | Any helper |
| Deep breathing and cough | **10 breaths every hour** while awake | Casualty, coached |
| Clot prevention exercises | **10 repetitions every 2 h** | Casualty or helper |
| Range of motion, all free joints | Every **8 h**, 5-10 repetitions each | Helper |
| Bath, change linen, change old tape | Every **24 h**, spot-clean as needed | Two helpers |
| Food or nutrition drink (if alert) | Every **4-6 h**, or 5-6 small meals a day | Helper |
| Blood glucose (if not eating, and you have a meter) | Every **8 h** | You |
| Splint check (pulses, skin) | Every **6 h** conscious, every **2 h** unconscious | You (lesson 07.5) |
| Wounds and dressings | At least every **24 h** | You (lesson 07.5) |

### 1. Repositioning and padding

**Who needs it:** anyone who cannot turn themselves or has difficulty doing so. A casualty who can move should be reminded to shift position every 2 hours.

**The danger points:** back of the head, shoulder blades, elbows, sacrum and buttocks, hips (on the side), knees and ankles (where they touch each other), and **heels**.

**Steps for a turn (two people):**

1. Explain to the casualty. Check that every line, drain, urine tube and dressing has **slack** before you roll.
2. Remove the wedges and padding.
3. Roll the casualty onto one side together. If a spine injury is possible, **log roll**: one person holds the head in line with the body and calls the count; the others roll shoulders, hips and legs as one unit. JTS: keep log-roll precautions, but still turn the casualty.
4. While on the side: look at the sacrum, back, buttocks and back of the head; clean if soiled; smooth out every crease in the sheet.
5. Lay them back onto a **wedge** (folded blanket or pillow along the back) so the body is tilted about **30 degrees** from flat, not lying fully on the hip. The international pressure injury guideline prefers the 30-degree tilted side-lying position to the 90-degree side-lying position (EPUAP/NPIAP 2019).
6. Pad between the knees and between the ankles so bones do not rest on bones. Keep the arms off the abdomen.
7. **Float the heels**: a pillow or rolled blanket under the **calves** so the heels hang free and the weight lies along the calf, not under the knee (EPUAP/NPIAP 2019).
8. Head and neck in line with the spine. Head of the bed raised about **30 degrees** if the casualty is unconscious, to reduce pneumonia (JTS). Avoid sitting them higher than that for long: the higher the head, the more the body slides and shears the skin over the sacrum (EPUAP/NPIAP favours keeping the head as flat as possible, so 30 degrees is the compromise).
9. Next turn to the other side, then on the back, in rotation. Write each position and time on the sheet.

**Changing the sheet with the casualty in bed (JTS):** roll the casualty to one side; roll the old sheet lengthwise to the middle of the bed; lay the new sheet on the empty half and tuck its edge under the old roll; roll the casualty back over both rolls to the other side; pull the old sheet out and the new one through.

**Checking the skin: the blanch test.** Press a red area with a finger for a few seconds and release. Skin that goes pale and then returns to red is still healthy. **Red skin that does not blanch** is the first stage of a pressure injury. JTS: outline it with a marker, keep the casualty off that area until it recovers, or add padding if you cannot avoid it. Do not rub or massage it (EPUAP/NPIAP: avoid vigorously rubbing at-risk skin).

| Stage (NPIAP) | What you see | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Intact skin, red patch that does not blanch (darker on dark skin; may feel warm, firm or painful) | Offload completely, mark, recheck every turn |
| 2 | Shallow open sore or blister, pink wound bed | Offload; keep clean; cover with a non-stick dressing |
| 3-4 | Deeper crater, fat, tendon or bone visible | Offload; wound care (lesson 07.5); tell the receiving team |
| Deep tissue injury | Purple or maroon area under intact skin | Offload; it may open in days |

### 2. Skin care and washing

- **Full wash every 24 hours**, spot-clean whenever soiled (JTS). Washing is also your daily head-to-toe skin check.
- Keep the casualty covered; expose only the part you are washing, with a towel under it.
- **Face first, genitals last.** Wash, rinse and **dry** small areas at a time, especially skin folds, armpits, groin and under breasts.
- Never put a used cloth back into the clean water. Change cloths between body areas.
- Women's long hair: braid it at the **side**, not down the back (a braid under the head is a pressure point, JTS).
- After incontinence: clean promptly, use mild (not alkaline) soap, and protect the skin with a barrier such as petroleum jelly or zinc cream (EPUAP/NPIAP).
- Eyes of an unconscious casualty: make sure the lids are closed, clean them with a damp cloth from the inner corner outward, and keep them moist with eye lubricant if you have it (course rule: open, dry eyes ulcerate within days).

### 3. Mouth care

Good oral hygiene reduces the bacteria linked to pneumonia, whether or not the casualty is intubated (JTS).

- **Conscious:** brush teeth at least every 12 hours; help if they cannot.
- **Unconscious:** at least every 4 hours, with suction ready.

**Steps for an unconscious casualty (JTS):**

1. Head turned to the side, or the casualty on their side, so fluid runs out, not down.
2. Make a **padded tongue depressor**: wrap gauze around one end of a wooden spatula and tape it. Insert it only just past the teeth to hold the mouth open. **Never put your bare finger between the teeth** of a casualty who may bite.
3. Wrap gauze around a gloved finger. Moisten it with water or mouthwash; do not soak it.
4. Gently wipe the teeth, gums, tongue and roof of the mouth for about a minute, changing gauze as it gets dirty.
5. Suction any fluid (a 60 ml syringe on a 25 cm piece of IV tubing works as a manual suction, JTS).
6. Apply lip balm or petroleum jelly.

Some older videos use lemon-glycerin swabs. They dry the mouth and are no longer recommended in many nursing guidelines (course note); use water or mouthwash.

### 4. Urine and bowels without an indwelling catheter

The JTS advises removing an indwelling catheter when possible and using an **external condom catheter in men** or a **wicking device in women** instead, because indwelling catheters cause urinary tract infection. External devices under-read output (lesson 07.3).

| Situation | Minimum | Better | Best |
|---|---|---|---|
| Man, conscious | Bottle or cut-down bottle as a urinal, offered every 2-3 h | Commercial urinal | Same, with measured output |
| Man, unconscious or immobile | Pads, weighed | **Condom (external) catheter** to a bag | Indwelling catheter by a professional, only if needed for hourly measurement |
| Woman, conscious | Bedpan (or improvised, below) | Female urinal | Same, with measured output |
| Woman, unconscious or immobile | Pads, weighed | External wicking device or pad changed every 2 h | Indwelling catheter by a professional, only if needed |

**Applying a condom (external) catheter:**

1. Choose the right size (packets have a sizing guide). Check for latex allergy.
2. Wash the penis with soap and water and **dry it completely**. Trim long pubic hair at the base; do not shave (nicks).
3. Retract the foreskin to wash, then **return the foreskin to its normal position** before applying (a retracted foreskin can swell and trap the head of the penis).
4. Place the sheath on the head of the penis, leaving a gap of 1-2 cm at the tip so the tube does not rub, and unroll it along the shaft. Press gently for 10-15 seconds so the adhesive grips.
5. Connect to the drainage bag or bottle below the level of the bladder, with no kinks. Secure the tube to the thigh with some slack.
6. Check after 15 minutes and at every turn: colour of the penis, leaks, twisting. Change it daily with a wash and skin check.

**Bedpan:** roll the casualty to one side, place the pan against the buttocks (wide end toward the back for a standard pan), roll back onto it and raise the head of the bed a little if allowed. A slipper-style **fracture pan** is easier for someone who cannot lift the hips. Remove the same way, clean and dry the skin from front to back, and measure the urine (lesson 07.3).

**Bowels:** opioids constipate. MSF advises adding a laxative when opioid treatment lasts more than 48 hours: lactulose (adult 15-45 ml a day; child under 1 year 5 ml, 1-6 years 5-10 ml, 7-14 years 10-15 ml a day) or, if stools are soft, bisacodyl (adult 10-15 mg once daily; child over 3 years 5-10 mg). Both are sold over the counter in pharmacies. Record bowel movements on the sheet. Diarrhoea is a fluid loss to count (lesson 07.3) and a skin threat to manage.

### 5. Breathing, clots and moving

- **Deep breathing (JTS):** breathe in slowly through the nose, expanding the lower ribs; hold 3-5 seconds; breathe out slowly through pursed lips. Ten times every hour while awake, and cough if there are secretions. A splinted chest or abdominal wound hurts: give a pillow to hug against the wound and time it after pain relief.
- **Clot prevention, conscious (JTS):** foot pumps (toes up and back, then pointed), ankle circles, straight-leg raises or knee-to-chest, and shoulder rolls; 10 repetitions every 2 hours while awake. Avoid exercising a fractured or severely injured limb.
- **Clot prevention, unconscious (JTS):** hold the heel and alternately bend the foot down and up, 5-10 seconds each way, 10 times every 2 hours; and gentle massage of the leg from ankle to thigh, avoiding deep pressure behind the knee. Compression stockings or elastic bandages wrapped from the toes upward, leaving toes visible.
- **Signs of a clot:** a swollen, painful, warm, red or discoloured calf or thigh. Stop exercises and massage on that leg and call for advice: sudden breathlessness or chest pain afterward may be a clot in the lung.
- **Range of motion:** every 8 hours, move every uninjured joint through its range 5-10 times.
- **Sitting and walking:** if the casualty is allowed, sit them on the edge of the bed 2-3 times a day (JTS minimum), then in a chair, then walking with help.

### 6. Feeding

Nutrition matters even in three days: it supports healing and blunts the body's breakdown of muscle after injury (JTS).

**Who eats:** a casualty who is **alert, can sit up at least 30 degrees, swallows safely**, is not vomiting, and has **no evisceration, no penetrating abdominal wound and no surgery expected within hours** (lesson 04.1).

**Swallow check (course method):** sitting up, give a teaspoon of water three times, then a sip from a cup. Stop if they cough, choke, the voice turns wet or gurgly, or water runs from the mouth. Try again later; do not feed someone who fails.

**What and how often (JTS 2025):**

- **Minimum:** if alert, encourage food, a protein shake or an oral nutrition supplement every 4-6 hours.
- **Better:** the casualty eats 50-70% of meals; **5-6 small meals** a day are often better tolerated than 3 large ones; high-protein items.
- **Best:** at least 75% of meals eaten, with supplements.

Do not chase full calories in the first days or force food on someone who is nauseated: small, frequent, protein-rich and well-hydrated is the target (course reasoning). A person who had already eaten little or nothing for many days before the injury is at risk of a dangerous salt shift when feeding restarts (refeeding syndrome): start them slowly and get medical advice.

**Blood sugar:** if the casualty is not eating and you have a meter, check every 8 hours. Below **80 mg/dl** (4.4 mmol/l) treat at once with oral sugar or juice if they can swallow (JTS).

**Tube feeding** (nasogastric) is a physician or advanced-provider procedure; the JTS describes it for intubated casualties held 48-72 hours.

## Improvise it

### Pressure relief with household padding

| Item | Use | What fails / risk | How to tell it is failing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Camping foam mat, yoga mat, folded duvet or sleeping bag under the whole body | Raise the casualty off a floor, door or table | Thin foam compresses fully ("bottoms out") | **Hand check (course method):** slide your flat hand palm-up under the padding beneath the sacrum; if you can feel the bone with less than a finger's thickness of padding, add a layer |
| Pillows, couch cushions, rolled towels | Wedges for the 30° tilt, between knees and ankles, under calves to float heels | Slide out within an hour | Wedge found loose at the next turn: tape it to the sheet or tuck it under |
| Lawn chair or folding lounger (JTS mentions a trifold lawn chair) | Holds head and legs raised | Hard bars at the hips | Red marks over the bars at each turn |
| Sheepskin, thick towel under heels | Reduces friction | Does not offload | Still red heels: float them instead |

Avoid rings or doughnut cushions and water-filled gloves under heels (course rule: they concentrate pressure on the ring of tissue around the bone).

### Washing with very little water

- **Two-bowl method:** one bowl of warm soapy water, one of clean water; a fresh cloth (or a fresh section of the cloth) for each area. About 2 litres is enough for a full wash.
- **Rolled-bandage method (JTS):** dip two gauze rolls in clean water; dip one in soapy water and wrap around a gloved hand; keep unrolling clean gauze as you wash, then rinse the same way with the second roll.
- **Baby wipes:** rinse them in water first. Most contain alcohol and residues that irritate skin (JTS).
- Hands: soap and water, or alcohol hand gel (at least 60% alcohol, course note) before and after every contact.

### Mouth care without supplies

Gauze or a clean cotton cloth on a finger (with a padded spatula, a wooden spoon wrapped in cloth, between the teeth). Rinse solution: a glass of clean water with half a teaspoon of salt or of baking soda (course suggestion). Lip balm: petroleum jelly or a drop of cooking oil. Suction: a 60 ml syringe on any soft tube.

### Urinals, bedpans and wicking

- **Male urinal:** a 1.5 L plastic bottle with a wider hole cut in the shoulder, edges taped smooth.
- **Female urinal:** a wide-mouthed plastic container (a large yoghurt tub or a cut bottle with a broad, smooth, taped rim) held against the vulva while the woman sits or lies on her side. Messy; put a towel underneath.
- **Bedpan:** a low baking tray or washing-up bowl with a rolled towel taped around the rim; or a bucket lined with a plastic bag, the woman supported over it if she can sit. Hard edges cause pressure sores within an hour: never leave anyone on a pan.
- **Improvised condom catheter:** a non-lubricated condom with the tip cut and a short soft tube (IV tubing, aquarium tubing) sealed into it with tape, draining to a bottle. **Risk:** tape wrapped all the way round the penis can strangle it as it swells. Use only a loose spiral of tape that does not meet itself, check colour every hour, and remove at once if dusky or swollen.
- **Wicking:** a folded incontinence pad or nappy against the vulva, changed and **weighed** every 2 hours (1 g = 1 ml, lesson 07.3). Clean the skin at every change.

### Homemade oral liquid diet

For a casualty who can swallow but cannot chew or eat much (course recipes; calories are approximate):

| Recipe | Ingredients | About |
|---|---|---|
| Energy shake | 250 ml UHT milk, 2 heaped tablespoons milk powder, 1 tablespoon sugar or honey, 1 tablespoon peanut butter or tahini, 1 ripe banana, blended or mashed and shaken | 450-500 kcal, 15-20 g protein |
| Salty soup | Chicken or lentil soup, blended smooth, with salt | Fluid, salt, some protein |
| Porridge | Oats or semolina cooked thin in milk, with sugar | Easy energy |
| Oral rehydration between meals | Lesson 11.2 recipe | Salt and water |

The JTS lists ultra-heat-treated milk, alone or with protein powder, as an improvised tube feed. Food safety: make shakes fresh, keep covered, and throw away anything left at room temperature for more than about 2 hours (course rule). **No honey for infants under 1 year** (botulism). No raw eggs.

## When it goes wrong

| Problem | Cause | Recovery |
|---|---|---|
| Red heel or sacrum that does not blanch on day 2 | Turns skipped at night, heels not floated, creased sheet | Mark it, offload completely, shorten the interval, write turn times on the sheet |
| Wet cough, fever, faster breathing on day 2-3 | Aspiration or pneumonia: dirty mouth, flat position, feeding a drowsy casualty | Head up 30°, mouth care every 4 h, stop oral intake until assessed, call (module 8) |
| Swollen, dusky penis | Condom catheter too tight, foreskin left retracted | Remove at once, return the foreskin, call if it does not settle |
| Painful swollen calf | Possible clot | Stop exercises on that leg, call for advice |
| Choking during a meal | Swallow not checked, casualty too flat | Stop, sit up, clear the airway (EMT skills), no more food until reassessed |
| Skin breakdown in the groin | Moisture from urine, wipes with alcohol | Clean promptly, dry, barrier cream, rinse wipes |
| Helpers stop turning on day 3 | Fatigue, no schedule | Turn schedule with names and times; the leader checks it at every mini-round |

## Scope and law

Nursing care, hygiene, positioning, oral care, feeding a conscious person and applying external urine devices are lay caregiving and within EMT scope in Israel, and teaching family and neighbours to do them is good practice. Inserting an indwelling urinary catheter or a feeding tube, and prescribing laxatives beyond over-the-counter use, belong to nurses, paramedics or physicians. The Duty to Rescue Law (1998) gives no immunity for how you help; see [01.5 · The law and your scope in Israel](../module-01/lesson-05.md).

## Practice

> [!WARNING]
> Practise turning only with a willing adult partner who has no back or neck problems, and never put fingers between the teeth of anyone during mouth-care practice: use the padded spatula. Practise the condom catheter on a model (a cucumber or a banana works) not on a person.

**Drill: two hours of nursing on a partner (90 minutes).** A partner lying on a floor mat, a helper, pillows, towels, a duvet, a marker, gauze, a wooden spatula and tape, two bowls, a timer, the flowsheet.

1. **Plan (10 min):** write a 2-hour nursing schedule for your "unconscious" partner with times and names.
2. **Turn (20 min):** with the helper, turn to the 30° tilt with a wedge, pad knees and ankles, float the heels. Do one log roll with the helper holding the head. Do the hand check under the sacrum on the bare floor, then with your padding.
3. **Change the sheet (10 min)** with the partner lying on it.
4. **Mouth care (10 min)** with the padded spatula held only at the front teeth, gauze on a gloved finger, water. Partner keeps eyes closed and does not help.
5. **Wash (20 min):** two-bowl method on face, arms and lower legs, using no more than 2 litres. Measure what you used.
6. **Model condom catheter (10 min)** on a cucumber with a condom and tube, using a spiral of tape.
7. **Skin check (10 min):** after your partner has lain still on one side for 20 minutes, look at the hip and do the blanch test.

How you know it worked: your partner says no bone was pressing at any point after padding; the hand check found padding at least a finger thick under the sacrum; the sheet change took under 5 minutes without your partner getting up; you used 2 litres or less for the wash; the tape on the model did not meet itself; and you wrote every turn time on the sheet.
